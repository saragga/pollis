# Snowball Search Map — Rounds and Saturation

## What a Round of Snowball Search Map Is
A round begins with seeds made after a list of citations, and ends with the lists following those seeds. Each new work that enters the top 20 of the pool in a round is marked **New in the top 20**. **Show All**, **New Since Last Step** and **Candidates** switch what the pool shows; Candidates are the top 20 works that are neither seeds nor excluded.

## When a Snowball Search Map Has Saturated
A search has saturated when a round adds nothing new to the top 20, and the page then says so: **nothing new in the top 20. The search has saturated.** Works keep entering lower down, as citation networks are very large, so the test looks at the top of the pool rather than at the whole of it. Saturation means that further rounds are unlikely to change what you would read first, not that every relevant work has been found.

## Using Run to Saturation
**Run to Saturation** follows the seeds not yet followed, then makes the best candidates seeds, round after round, until a round brings nothing new into the top 20. Choose **Seeds a round**, **Rounds at most**, up to 20, and **Following**: Cited By, Cites or both. **Stop** ends the search once the running round finishes. A run takes many requests, so a key for each index helps at busy times.

## Keeping a Work Out of the Top of the Pool
**Exclude** ranks a work after all others, and it never enters the top, so that an off-topic classic does not fill a place or hold back saturation. **Include** puts it back. A very cited methods paper is a common case, tied to every seed while saying little about the topic.

## Where the Idea of Saturation in a Snowball Search Comes From
Saturation is borrowed from qualitative research, where it means that further data are unnecessary. [Saunders, Sim, Kingstone, Baker, Waterfield, Bartlam, Burroughs and Jinks (2018)](#ref-SSKBW...2018a) distinguish four approaches to it and report uncertainty over how it should be conceptualised, which is a caution against treating the word as a guarantee. Snowball Search Map avoids that uncertainty by testing one stated thing, the top of the pool, and its message should be read for what it tests.
