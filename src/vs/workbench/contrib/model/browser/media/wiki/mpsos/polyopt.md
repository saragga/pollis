# Polynomial Programming: Polynomial Optimisation

The **Polynomial Optimisation** problem of the panel: a lower bound on the minimum of p over a set described by polynomial inequalities, found through a certificate in which sums of squares multiply the constraints.

## The problem

The panel minimises p(x) over a semialgebraic set

K = {x : g_1(x) ≥ 0, ..., g_m(x) ≥ 0}

the setting of [Laurent (2009)](#ref-Lau2009a), who notes that the problem is NP-hard in general. **Domain** describes K in the `@set` syntax, and its default `sum(x .^ 2) <= 4` is the ball of radius 2, the single inequality 4 − x_1² − ... − x_n² ≥ 0. Several inequalities are joined with `&&`, as in `x[1] >= 0 && x[2] >= 0`.

[Lasserre (2001)](#ref-Las2001a) shows that finding the global minimum of a polynomial, on R^n or on a compact set defined by polynomial inequalities, reduces to solving an often finite sequence of convex linear matrix inequality problems. The panel solves one member of that sequence, chosen by **Relaxation Degree**.

## A certificate on a set

Non-negativity of p − t on K is relaxed by asking for sums of squares s_0, s_1, ..., s_m with

p − t = s_0 + s_1 g_1 + ... + s_m g_m

On K every term on the right is non-negative, so any t with such a representation is a lower bound on the minimum. [Laurent (2009)](#ref-Lau2009a) explains that the degrees of the s_j must be bounded to obtain a semidefinite program, since terms may cancel on the right, and bounds deg(s_0) and every deg(s_j g_j) by an even number 2k. The best bound of that order is then one semidefinite program, and the bounds can only improve as k grows.

## How SumOfSquares.jl writes it

The constraint of the code is

`cref = @constraint(m, p >= t, domain = S, maxdegree = d)`

with `S = @set ...` built from **Domain** and t maximised. The SumOfSquares.jl documentation calls the result a Putinar certificate: sums of squares σ_i are searched so that the degree of each σ_i g_i does not exceed `maxdegree`. **Relaxation Degree** sets d, the 2k of the previous section.

Left unset, `maxdegree` would default, as the documentation explains, to the largest degree among the monomials and the multipliers of the domain, so that at least constant multipliers can be used. [Legat, Weisser, Lofberg and Parrilo (2023)](#ref-LWLP2023a) wrote the package.

## Putinar's theorem

When does the bound reach the minimum? [Laurent (2009)](#ref-Lau2009a) calls the set of all sums s_0 + Σ s_j g_j the quadratic module generated by the g_j, and calls it Archimedean when, for every polynomial f, some N makes N ± f a member. Putinar's theorem then states that every polynomial positive on K belongs to the module. As Laurent notes, following [Lasserre (2001)](#ref-Las2001a), the bounds of the hierarchy then converge to the minimum.

The condition holds, Laurent points out, when one of the constraints alone defines a compact set, as the default ball does. When K is known to lie in a ball of radius R, adding the redundant constraint R² − x_1² − ... − x_n² ≥ 0 to **Domain** gives a description that satisfies it.

## Global and local answers

The SumOfSquares.jl tutorial on polynomial optimisation contrasts the two approaches on a cubic in two variables over a polyhedron. A local solver, which uses only values and derivatives at the current point, stops at a local minimum, while the sum of squares relaxation gives a lower bound equal to the global minimum, and its moment matrix yields the global minimisers.

**SOS vs Sampling** makes the same comparison with random points: the smallest sampled value of p in K is an upper bound on the minimum, the certified bound a lower one. **Extract Minimiser** reads the minimisers from the moment matrix, as [Lasserre Hierarchy](lasserre.md) and [Duality](duality.md) explain.
