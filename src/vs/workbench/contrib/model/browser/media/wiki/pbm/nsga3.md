# Pareto-Based Multi-Objective Optimisation: NSGA-III

**NSGA-III**, the reference-point-based algorithm of [Deb and Jain (2014)](#ref-DJ2014a), keeps the framework of NSGA-II but replaces the crowding distance with closeness to a set of reference points, for problems with many objectives.

## Many objectives

[Deb and Jain (2014)](#ref-DJ2014a) observe that evolutionary multi-objective algorithms had shown their niche on practical problems with mostly two and three objectives, and that there was a growing need for algorithms handling many-objective problems, which they define as having four or more objectives. They review recent efforts and discuss viable directions before proposing their algorithm.

The panel's decision table follows the same division: **NSGA-II** for two or three objectives, **NSGA-III** for four or more.

## Reference points

NSGA-III follows the NSGA-II framework and emphasises population members that are non-dominated, yet close to a set of supplied reference points, in the words of [Deb and Jain (2014)](#ref-DJ2014a). Non-dominated sorting still ranks the merged population; the reference points take the place of crowding as the second criterion.

[Emmerich and Deutz (2024)](#ref-ED2024a) place NSGA-III among the decomposition-based algorithms, which split a problem into subproblems that each target a different section of the Pareto front, and note that NSGA-III, designed for many-objective optimisation, uses dynamically updated reference points.

## Results of the original paper

[Deb and Jain (2014)](#ref-DJ2014a) apply NSGA-III to many-objective test problems with three to 15 objectives and compare it with two versions of MOEA/D. Each MOEA/D version works well on different classes of problems, while NSGA-III is found to produce satisfactory results on all the problems considered.

Their paper treats unconstrained problems with box constraints, as its title says; a sequel considers constrained problems and other specialties of many-objective optimisation.

## In Metaheuristics.jl

`NSGA3` takes the same population size and variation parameters as `NSGA2`, with two more: `reference_points`, usually generated by `gen_ref_dirs`, and `partitions`, the number of partitions used to generate the reference points when none are given, 12 by default. The Metaheuristics.jl guide recommends `NSGA3` for many-objective problems, with more than three objectives, and lists it among its choices for constrained multi-objective problems.

## In the panel

The main code calls `NSGA3(N = N, options = ...)` with **Population** as N and the default partitions. With the default two-objective problem, **Compare Algorithms** sets NSGA-III against the other three algorithms on the same problem, budget and seed.

For four or more objectives, **Objectives** takes a longer vector, and **Pareto Front Plot** shows the first three objectives in 3D. **Trade-Off Rates** needs exactly two objectives, and `SMS_EMOA`, as its documentation states, approximates the hypervolume by sampling when there are more than two.
