# Discover Literature — Overview

## Where the Results of Discover Literature Come From
Discover Literature asks two free, open indexes of scholarly works. OpenAlex is built from open sources of metadata ([Priem, Piwowar and Orr (2022)](#ref-PPO2022a)), and Semantic Scholar adds a reading of the citations themselves. Used together, the two find more: each lists its 200 most cited for the search, and the two lists are merged, sorted by the larger of the two **Since Publishing** counts. Semantic Scholar's fields are broader than OpenAlex's, and Influence and 2-Year Mean Citedness are OpenAlex's.

## Why One Article Shows Two Citation Counts
No index holds every citation, so the same article has different counts in each. The results keep both, under the headings OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar. An article found by one index alone shows nothing under the other until **Reconcile Sources** asks each source for the counts of the articles the other alone found. The buttons **OpenAlex (n)** and **Semantic Scholar (n)** say how many are waiting.

## What the Versions of an Article Are
**+n versions** beneath an article lists its other records, such as a preprint or a repository copy, each with its own counts. Those counts are never added up, because one citation may have gone to both records. An abstract is fetched on demand from either index, and **Read** follows the link to the article.

## What Each Action on Ticked Articles Does

| Action | What it does | Saves anything |
|---|---|---|
| See in References | Shows those already in PollisReferences in Browse References | No |
| Show in Panel... | Adds them to a panel's References | On confirming |
| Copy BibTeX | Copies their entries | No |
| Open PDFs | Opens open-access copies in the internal browser | No |
| Snowball Search... | Makes them seeds of a map | No, kept in the map |
| Save to PollisReferences | Saves them as your works | Yes |

## How Much the Coverage of Scholarly Indexes Differs
Comparisons of the large indexes show how far they part. [Visser, van Eck and Waltman (2021)](#ref-VVW2021a) compare Scopus, Web of Science, Dimensions, Crossref and Microsoft Academic over 2008 to 2017, and find that both coverage and citation links differ. [Martín-Martín, Thelwall, Orduna-Malea and Delgado López-Cózar (2021)](#ref-MTOD2021a) collect 3,073,351 citations to 2,515 highly cited documents of 2006, of which Google Scholar finds 88%. A count from Discover Literature is comparable with others from the same index, not across indexes.

## How Common Open-Access Copies Are When Using Open PDFs
**Open PDFs** in Discover Literature can only open the copies that exist. [Piwowar, Priem, Larivière, Alperin, Matthias, Norlander, Farley, West and Haustein (2018)](#ref-PPLAM...2018a) estimate that at least 28% of the scholarly literature is open access, and the most common mechanism is Bronze: free to read at the publisher's page, without a clear licence. Open-access articles in their sample receive 18% more citations than average. An article with no open copy found may still be available through a library.
