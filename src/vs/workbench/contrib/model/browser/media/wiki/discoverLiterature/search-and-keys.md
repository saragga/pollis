# Discover Literature — Searching and Keys

## How to Write a Topic in Discover Literature
**Topic** searches in **Title and Abstract** or in **Title**, as chosen in Search In. AND or a space takes all the words, as in `bootstrap AND jackknife`. OR takes either, as in `bootstrap OR jackknife`. Quotes keep a phrase together. A broad topic returns only the 200 most cited of each index, so adding a word or narrowing the years brings up articles that would otherwise be cut off.

## The Journal Ranking Choices of Discover Literature

| Choice | Source | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Any Journal | | Nothing |
| 2-Year Mean Citedness Above | OpenAlex's measure, close to an impact factor | Nothing |
| Norwegian register's levels | The PollisJournals database | The database |
| SJR quartiles | SCImago's file | Databases: Import Journal Metrics... |

The last two are checked on the articles listed, not in the search itself, so a page of results may shrink.

## Do You Need an OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar Key
Neither key is required. Without one, OpenAlex shares a free budget of about 100 searches with everyone on your network, and Semantic Scholar shares a pool that at busy times asks you to wait. **Get a Free Key** leads to each provider: OpenAlex gives one for your email, and Semantic Scholar through a form asking your name, email and use. **Set Key...** stores it in the system's keychain, and **Change Key...** replaces it.
