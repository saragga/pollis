# Larousse Through the Editions — Overview

## What Larousse Through the Editions Shows
The **Larousse Through the Editions** page follows an article through the encyclopaedic dictionaries of Larousse from 1866 to 1930: the Grand dictionnaire universel du XIXe siècle of Pierre Larousse, of 1866 to 1876, and its two supplements, of 1878 and 1890; the Nouveau Larousse illustré, of 1897 to 1904, and its supplement of 1906; and the second and third volumes of the Larousse du XXe siècle, of 1929 and 1930. For each work it shows the article as the scan of that work prints it: its title, its length in characters, its characters per million characters of the work, its full text, and a link to its page of the scan at the Internet Archive. Side by side, the works show which subjects were given an article, how much room each was given, and how the text changed from one work to the next. The page opens from Extend > Historical Geography > Encyclopedias > Larousse, from PollisLarousse in the Databases view, and from the Command Palette.

## Looking Up a Headword in Larousse Through the Editions
**Headword** looks up an article in every work of Larousse and its supplements. Type the headword as the Larousse prints it, such as `HUMBOLDT` or `PASTEUR`, without the first names or the qualifier in brackets that follow it, and press **Look Up** or Enter; case and accents do not matter, so `ÉCOLE` and `ecole` find the same articles. Words after a comma narrow the articles to those whose title has them: `DARWIN, CHARLES` finds the articles on Charles Darwin and leaves out those on Erasmus Darwin. When no work has an article under the headword, the page says so and offers the headwords that start the same letters, each a link that looks it up.

## The Bars: Per Million Characters and Length in Characters
Above the works, a bar for each work measures the longest article found under the headword. **Per Million Characters of the Work** gives how many of every million characters of all the articles of its work the article takes up, which allows works of different sizes to be compared: the Grand dictionnaire universel fills fifteen volumes, the Nouveau Larousse illustré seven. **Length in Characters** gives the length of the article itself, counting letters, spaces and punctuation as the scan reads them. At the left of each bar are the **Work**, by its key, such as `GDU` or `NLI-S`, and its **Years**; the pointer on the key shows the work's name, and the pointer on a value shows the title of the article measured.

## Works Without the Article in Larousse Through the Editions
A full work with no article under the headword has an empty bar and a dash. The supplements, GDU-S1, GDU-S2 and NLI-S, added articles to the work before them, whose volumes their readers kept: an article they leave out was still in those volumes, so their bars say **Not in this supplement** in place of the dash. Of the Larousse du XXe siècle, L20, only the second and third of its six volumes are read; a headword whose letters fall outside them says **Not in the volumes read**, and the pointer on it shows the first and last headwords of those volumes. The line above the bars counts the full works and the supplements that have the article apart, and leaves out the Larousse du XXe siècle when the headword falls outside its volumes read.

## Each Work's Card in Larousse Through the Editions
Below the bars, a card for each work names it and its years, then gives the longest article under the headword: its title with its first names or qualifier, its characters, its characters per million of the work, and its volume and page of the scan. Articles of Larousse are not signed, so the cards name no contributor. **Open the Scan** opens that page of the scan as an image in Pollis's own browser, beside the page, at the size it was scanned. The tab is titled with the work, the volume and the page, and the browser's bar has **Previous Page** and **Next Page** to read on through the volume. **Open the Scan In**, below the headword, chooses **A New Tab** for each page or **The Same Tab**; Pollis keeps the choice. **Open in the Internet Archive** opens the page in the archive's reader. Other articles under the same headword, such as a second person of the same surname or a place named after a person, are listed under **Also under this headword**.

## Reading the Text of an Article in Larousse Through the Editions
**Show the Text** on a work's card opens the full text of its article below the card, a paragraph at a time, and **Hide the Text** closes it. The texts are those the Internet Archive read from the scans, with the running heads and page numbers left out and the words hyphenated at the end of a line joined, so they carry the misread letters of the scan; the figures and plates of the Nouveau Larousse illustré are not in them, and **Open the Scan** shows the pages as printed. The texts of all the articles come with PollisLarousse.jl as a download of their own, about 250 MB, which Pollis makes the first time a text is shown on the page: the card says **Downloading the texts of the Larousses** while it runs, and Julia keeps the texts on the computer afterwards.

## The PollisLarousse Database and Query in SQL
The page reads PollisLarousse, a built-in database that Pollis makes when the Julia REPL starts from the files of PollisLarousse.jl, and makes again when the package is updated. Its tables are `editions`, a row per work, and `articles`, a row per article of each work. In a duckdb> terminal they are `PollisLarousse.articles` and `PollisLarousse.editions`, and in Julia `larousse` once connected from the Databases view. The Next Steps group **Query in SQL** has four queries to start from: An Article Through the Works, Longest Articles of a Work, New Headwords of a Supplement and Articles by Letter.
