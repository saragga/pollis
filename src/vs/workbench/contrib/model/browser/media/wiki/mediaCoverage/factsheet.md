# Media Coverage — Factsheet

**Media Coverage** counts the news articles about a company, an institution or a subject over a period, charts them by hour, day or week, and lists their headlines, newest first.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | See how much the news said about something, and when |
| **Search** | Words, a Yahoo Finance ticker, or both |
| **Sources** | Google News, Yahoo Finance, The New York Times, The Guardian, PR Newswire |
| **Period** | Last 24 Hours to Last Year, or From a Day to a Day |
| **Marks** | FOMC Releases, SEC Filings of the Ticker, or A Time |
| **Results** | Coverage, a bar chart of the articles; Articles, a table of When, From, Headline and Source |
| **Menu** | Explore > Media Coverage; also the News of an event in Event Studies |

## When to Use Media Coverage
- Seeing whether a company was in the news before or after a filing, an announcement or a fall in its price.
- Reading what was written about the Federal Reserve around an FOMC statement.
- Comparing how much coverage a subject had in one week and in another.
- Finding the press release behind a result announced on the newswires.

## What Media Coverage Does Not Do
The Media Coverage page shows headlines and links only: the articles themselves open in the internal browser, at their outlets. It does not score the tone of an article or tell positive news from negative. The counts are those of the articles each source lists, so a source that caps its answers, such as Google News at 100 articles a search, leaves gaps in a long period.
