# Knowledge Base — Overview

The **Knowledge Base** is a collection of openly licensed texts that a model can answer from: books, lecture notes, reports and manuals, chosen for the subjects of Pollis. Its texts come from `PollisKnowledge.jl` and from the toolboxes installed, each with its licence, and the page switches them on, downloads them and builds their index, as Retrieval-Augmented Generation does for a collection of your own documents.

## What the Knowledge Base Holds
A text is in the Knowledge Base only when its licence lets it be shared. Each row of the table names a text with its **Type**, such as Book, its **Source**, `PollisKnowledge.jl`, a toolbox or User, its **Subject**, its **Key Words** and **Subject Classifications**, its **Licence**, its **Size** as downloaded, what is kept of it (**As Kept**), how it is cut into **Passages**, and its switch, **In the Knowledge Base**. The books include Dive into Deep Learning ([Zhang, Lipton, Li and Smola (2023)](#ref-ZLLS2023a)), Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction ([Murphy (2022)](#ref-Mur2022a)), Algorithms for Optimization ([Kochenderfer and Wheeler (2027)](#ref-KW2027a)) and Algorithms for Decision Making ([Kochenderfer, Wheeler and Wray (2022)](#ref-KWW2022a)). **Authors' Page** opens the page of a text on its authors' site, and a click on the title of a text on this computer opens it.

## Key Words and Subject of the Texts of PollisKnowledge.jl
The **Key Words** and **Subject Classifications** of a text of `PollisKnowledge.jl` come from its work in `PollisReferences.jl`, the library of references of Pollis: those its PDF prints, else those OpenAlex or arXiv give. An article about Julia packages names them first in its **Subject**, before the class of its first code, such as Manifolds.jl; Software and its engineering (ACM CCS), and a search for a package finds the texts about it. A text whose work gives none has none.

## Your Own Texts in the Knowledge Base
Your own books, articles and reports are in the Knowledge Base when they are in the `knowledge_base` folder of the Pollis Workspace open: PDF, Markdown and text files. They are in the same table as the texts of `PollisKnowledge.jl`, with **Source** User, the path of the file under the title, and the **Type** of their folder: Book in `knowledge_base/books`, Article in `knowledge_base/articles`, Report in `knowledge_base/reports`, and Other elsewhere in `knowledge_base`. A text of yours is switched **On** until you switch it **Off**, and is in the index once **Build the Index** has run again, in the same index as the other texts. A file put in the folder, or taken out of it, shows in the table at once. A folder named otherwise in the `[folders]` table of `pollis.toml` is the one read.

## Texts a Toolbox Adds to the Knowledge Base
A toolbox installed can add texts to the Knowledge Base: lecture notes, books or articles whose authors share them under an open licence. They are in the same table, with the toolbox as their **Source**, such as Finance Toolbox, which adds Lecture Notes in Empirical Asset Pricing (PhD), and they leave the table when the toolbox is uninstalled. The toolbox ships no PDF: it names where the authors keep the text, at a fixed version. **On** downloads the PDF from there the first time, checks that it is the file the toolbox names, by its SHA-256, and keeps it with a `LICENCE.md` in `~/.pollis/knowledge/toolboxes`. A download that fails, or a file that is not the one named, leaves the text **Off**, and the row says why. **Authors' Page** opens the page of the text on its authors' site.

## Adding and Renaming Your Texts
**Add Your Texts...**, above the table, adds texts **From This Computer...** or **From a Link or DOI...**. From This Computer opens the files of your computer in the Downloads folder, where a text just downloaded is: choose one or more PDF, Markdown or text files, then their type, Book, Article or Report, and write the title of each: Pollis copies the file into `knowledge_base/books`, `knowledge_base/articles` or `knowledge_base/reports`, named by its title, and leaves the file you chose where it was. A colon of the title becomes a dash in the file's name. When a file of that name is there already, Pollis asks whether to replace it or keep both. The title of a text of yours is the name of its file, as the table and the sources of an answer of Ask Pollis show it: **Rename...**, under the path of a text of yours, renames its file by the title you write. From a Link or DOI takes a link to a PDF, the page of an article, a DOI or an arXiv identifier, such as `arXiv:2106.11342`: Pollis downloads the PDF of arXiv, the open copy OpenAlex knows of for a DOI, or the PDF an article's page links to, then asks its type and title. When no open copy can be downloaded, as for an article behind a paywall, **Open Its Page** opens it in the browser, to download it with your library's access and add it From This Computer. Without a Pollis Workspace open, the link reads **Open a Pollis Workspace to Add Your Texts...** and opens one.

## Subjects of the Books, from the Library of Congress
The **Subject** of a book of `PollisKnowledge.jl` is given by the Library of Congress: its subject headings, LCSH, as the record of the book has them, such as Machine learning; Probabilities (LCSH). **Subject Classifications** has its call number in the Library of Congress Classification, LCC, such as LCC: Q325.5 .M872 2022. **Library of Congress Record**, beside **Authors' Page**, opens the record they come from, and the cells name its number, the LCCN. A book whose edition has no record yet takes them from the record of an earlier edition, and the cells say which.

## Your Articles with Their LaTeX Source
The text of a PDF, as Pollis reads it for the passages, loses its mathematics: a formula comes out as scattered symbols, which a model answers from badly. An article of arXiv has its LaTeX source too, and a PDF with a `.tex` file of the same name beside it, in the `knowledge_base` folder, is one text whose passages come from its source: cut at its sections, without its comments, labels, figures but their captions, and bibliography, the mathematics as written, `$\sigma^2$`, which models read well. A passage found opens the PDF, at the page of its section, by the PDF's bookmarks. **From a Link or DOI...** downloads the source of an arXiv article with its PDF, its files in one, and **LaTeX Source**, under the path of a PDF of yours, downloads it from arXiv by the identifier its first page or the name of its file has, which you confirm. The key words and classifications a source states, `\keywords{...}` and `\subjclass[2020]{...}` say, come before what its first page prints. A rename takes the PDF and its source together.

## Key Words, Subject and Subject Classifications of Your Texts
Many articles print their key words and subject classifications on their first page, such as "Key words. Julia, numerical, scientific computing, parallel" and "AMS subject classifications. 68N15, 65Y05, 97P40". Pollis reads them when a text of yours comes into the `knowledge_base` folder: **Key Words** lists the key words, and **Subject Classifications** each classification system with its codes, the primary first: MSC, the Mathematics Subject Classification, JEL, the classification of the Journal of Economic Literature, or ACM CCS, the ACM Computing Classification System. A text that prints none has none. **Subject** is the top-level class of the first code, as its system names it, with the code of the class beside it: 68N15 gives Computer science (MSC 68). Pollis keeps them in `knowledge_base/catalogue.toml`, one `[[texts]]` table a text, by its path in the folder. A click on a cell of a text of yours changes them; a subject left empty is that of the first code again.

## Finding a Text in the Knowledge Base
The texts are in one table, whatever their type. The type menu beside the search box keeps one **Type**, such as Book, Report or Article, and says how many texts each has; **All Types** shows them all. The search box keeps the texts whose title, authors, edition, publisher, type, source, subject, key words, subject classifications, licence or path have its words. A click on a heading sorts the table by that column, and a second click reverses it. **Rows per page** sets how many texts a page of the table shows, with **Previous** and **Next** between pages. The cards of **With Thanks to Their Authors** show the books the type and the search leave.

## The Printed Books, With Thanks to Their Authors
The books of the Knowledge Base are open because their authors made them so, and each is also a printed book. Above the table, **With Thanks to Their Authors** shows a card for each: its cover, as its authors show it on their page, its title, edition, authors, publisher and year. A click on the cover, or on **Authors' Page**, opens the book's page on its authors' site, and **Hard Copy** opens the page of its publisher, where the printed book is sold. The covers come with `PollisKnowledge.jl` and belong to their publishers.

## Licences of the Texts in the Knowledge Base
The **Licence** column links to the text of each licence. A text under CC BY-NC-ND, no derivatives, is kept as the PDF its authors distribute, unchanged. A text under CC BY-SA, share alike, may be changed, and the changes are shared under the same licence: Dive into Deep Learning is kept as its Markdown sources, one file a section, with its code left out, so that a model answering from it is not led to Python where Pollis works in Julia. **As Kept** says what was changed. Each downloaded text carries `LICENCE.md`, with its authors, source, changes and licence.

## Switching a Text On and Off
**On** downloads a text of `PollisKnowledge.jl` the first time, from the release that holds it, and keeps it in the folder of Julia artifacts, `~/.julia/artifacts`; the row shows **Downloading...** meanwhile. **Off** leaves the text on the computer and out of the next index. **Switch On All Shown** and **Switch Off All Shown** do the same for every text the type and the search leave, on every page of the table, downloading one text at a time. A text switched on is in the index only once **Build the Index** has run again, and the page says when the texts switched on have changed since the last build.

## How a Text Is Cut into Passages
A text in PDF is read as one document, cut at its bookmarks and then into passages of about **Passage Length** words, at its paragraphs. A text from Markdown is one document a section, cut at its headings. A text of yours with its LaTeX source is cut at the sections of the source. Each passage keeps the heading it is under, so a passage found names the chapter and section it comes from. The **Passages** column says how each text is cut, and how many of its passages the index holds once it is built.

## Building the Index of the Knowledge Base
**Embedding Model** chooses the model that embeds the passages, by default that of Ask Pollis, and **Passage Length** their length, from 100 to 500 words. **Build the Index** reads the texts switched on, cuts and embeds them into `~/.pollis/rag/knowledge-base.duckdb`. Each passage is embedded once by each model, ever, so building again after switching another text on embeds only the new passages. The page says when the index was embedded with another model or cut to another length than the ones chosen.

## Asking Ask Pollis from the Knowledge Base
Once its index is built, the **Knowledge Base** is listed in **Search In** of Ask Pollis, after the sources of Pollis and the Julia Manual. Ticked alone, it is the only thing searched. Ticked with sources of Pollis, each is searched in its own index and gives part of the passages: most come from the index nearest the question when both were embedded with the same model, and from Pollis otherwise. **Auto** does not search the Knowledge Base; tick it, or choose **All Sources**. A passage of a PDF opens at its page in the PDF Viewer, and a passage of Dive into Deep Learning at its heading in the Markdown preview.

## Common Workflow
1. Read the **Licence** and **As Kept** of a text, and switch it **On**.
2. Keep the default **Passage Length**, 250 words, to begin with.
3. Choose the **Embedding Model** and **Build the Index**.
4. Tick **Knowledge Base** in **Search In** of Ask Pollis, ask a question the text answers, and follow the passages it cites.
5. Switch on more texts, and **Build the Index** again.
