# Systematic Review: Two Screeners and Their Agreement

Why two screeners screen every record in Systematic Review, how Cohen's kappa measures their agreement, and how the records they disagree on are settled.

## How Do the Screeners of Systematic Review Decide a Record?

A screener of Systematic Review reads each record's title and abstract and decides, against the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the Question step, whether the record goes on to the full text: include, exclude or unsure. Screening keeps a record that may meet the criteria, for its full text to decide, so a screener in doubt includes it, and a record with no abstract whose title does not rule it out is unsure. For an excluded record, the reason names the criterion it fails: the population, the intervention, the comparison, the outcome or the study design. Unsure counts as include on the page: a record goes on when a screener includes it or is unsure of it.

## What Is a Conflict in Systematic Review?

A conflict is a record that one screener of Systematic Review would keep, as include or unsure, and the other would exclude. A record both keep goes on to the full text; a record both exclude is excluded. The Conflicts step lists each conflict with both screeners' decisions and reasons, the third screener's decision once it has run, and your decision, Include or Exclude. A conflict is settled by your decision when you made one, else by the third screener's. Extraction cannot start while a conflict is unsettled, since the studies to extract are not known until every conflict is.

## What Does Cohen's Kappa Say About the Screeners?

The Conflicts step of Systematic Review shows two measures of how far the screeners agree on keeping or excluding records. **Agreement** is the share of records on which both screeners made the same choice. Two screeners who exclude most records agree on many of them by chance alone, so the share agreed says little by itself. **Cohen's Kappa**, the coefficient of agreement for nominal scales of [Cohen (1960)](#ref-Coh1960a), corrects for that: it is 1 when the screeners agree on every record, 0 when they agree no more than chance would have them, and below 0 when they agree less. A low kappa with clear criteria often points to criteria the screeners read differently, which the reasons in the conflicts show.

## When Should a Third Agent Settle the Conflicts?

**Settle with an Agent**, in the Conflicts step of Systematic Review, starts a third agent, on the agent and model chosen in **Third Screener**, with the records the screeners disagree on, both their decisions and reasons, and the review's criteria. It decides each as include or exclude, and its decision settles every conflict you have not decided yourself. At first, the third screener has an agent and model neither screener has, when there is one. A third agent suits many conflicts; a few are as quickly settled by reading them. Your decision always comes first, so you can let the agent settle them all and then change those you disagree with.
