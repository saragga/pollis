# BM25

BM25 scores a document against a query. It keeps the rarity of terms from TF-IDF, stops term counts from growing without limit, and adjusts for document length.

## Where BM25 Comes From

The probabilistic view asks how likely a document is to be relevant, given the terms it contains. [Robertson and Spärck Jones (1976)](#ref-RJ1976a) derived term weights from that question, using relevance information, and in its absence those weights reduce to a form close to idf. BM25, from the Okapi system, adds the term-frequency and length parts as practical approximations. [Robertson and Zaragoza (2009)](#ref-RZ2009a) give the full account and its variants. The method's success in competition made it a standard baseline.

## How the Score Is Computed

For each query term t, a document d with f occurrences of t and length |d| earns:

- idf(t) × f (k1 + 1) / (f + k1 (1 − b + b |d| / avgdl))

The document's score is the sum over the query terms. Here avgdl is the mean document length, and k1 and b are tuning parameters. Usual values are k1 from 1.2 to 2 and b = 0.75. In the panel, `bm_25(m)` applies its package defaults, and it returns a D × V matrix, so a score is a row of that matrix times the query counts.

## How Repeated Terms Saturate

The fraction rises with f, and it levels off at k1 + 1. For k1 = 1.2 and b = 0.75, at three document lengths:

| f | Half the mean length | Mean length | Twice the mean length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.257 | 1.000 | 0.710 |
| 2 | 1.600 | 1.375 | 1.073 |
| 10 | 2.047 | 1.964 | 1.818 |

Going from 1 to 2 uses adds far more than going from 2 to 10. The first mention says most of what a document has to say about a term.

## How Document Length Enters

Long documents use terms more often just because they are long. The factor 1 − b + b |d| / avgdl makes each use count for less in a long document. With b = 0, length is ignored, and with b = 1, counts are scaled fully by relative length. The value 0.75 sits between these. Set b lower when long documents really do cover more, such as in reports with many sections. Set it higher when length comes from repetition or padding.

## How the IDF Differs

BM25 usually uses a form with a probabilistic origin:

- idf(t) = ln((D − df + 0.5) / (df + 0.5))

For D = 100, a term in 50 documents gets 0, and a term in 60 gets about −0.40, so that a very common term can lower a score. Many systems avoid this by flooring at zero, or by adding 1 inside the logarithm. [Robertson (2004)](#ref-Rob2004a) examines the theoretical arguments for idf. Implementations differ on that detail, and they can rank documents differently when a query includes common terms.

## How BM25 Compares with TF-IDF

| Feature | TF-IDF with cosine | BM25 |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated terms | Linear, unless logged | Saturate |
| Length | Vector normalisation | Explicit, set by b |
| Settings | Choice of scheme | k1 and b |
| Symmetric | Yes, for two documents | No, it is query against document |

**TF-IDF vs BM25** ranks the corpus both ways, for one query. In the code, both are plain dot products, with no cosine step. Disagreement is common lower down the ranking, and top-ranked documents often overlap when a query has distinctive terms.
