# Britannica Through the Editions — Overview

## What Britannica Through the Editions Shows
The **Britannica Through the Editions** page follows an article of the Encyclopaedia Britannica through every edition from the first, published from 1768 to 1771, to the fourteenth, of 1929. For each edition it shows the article as the scan of that edition prints it: its title, its length in characters, its characters per million characters of the edition, the initials of its contributor where it is signed, its full text, and a link to its page of the scan at the Internet Archive. Side by side, the editions show which subjects were given an article, when, and how much room, and how its text changed from one edition to the next. The page opens from Extend > Historical Geography and Archaeology > Britannica Through the Editions, from PollisBritannica in the Databases view, and from the Command Palette.

## Looking Up a Headword in Britannica Through the Editions
**Headword** looks up an article in every edition and its supplements. Type the headword as the Britannica prints it, such as `PETRA` or `HUMBOLDT`, and press **Look Up** or Enter; case and accents do not matter. Words after a comma narrow the articles to those whose title has them: `DARWIN, CHARLES` finds the articles on Charles Darwin and leaves out those on Erasmus Darwin. When no edition has an article under the headword, the page says so and offers the headwords that start the same letters, each a link that looks it up. That no edition has an article is itself a finding: a subject the Britannica of those years did not treat on its own.

## The Bars: Per Million Characters and Length in Characters
Above the editions, a bar for each edition measures the longest article found under the headword. **Per Million Characters of the Edition** gives how many of every million characters of all the articles of its edition the article takes up, which allows editions of different sizes to be compared: the first edition fills three volumes, the eleventh twenty-eight. **Length in Characters** gives the length of the article itself, counting letters, spaces and punctuation as the scan reads them. At the left of each bar are the **Edition**, 1 to 14, or 3S and 6S for the supplements, and its **Years**; the pointer on the edition shows its name, and the pointer on a value shows the title of the article measured.

## Editions Without the Article in Britannica Through the Editions
A full edition with no article under the headword has an empty bar and a dash. The supplements, 3S and 6S, and the new volumes of the tenth, twelfth and thirteenth editions added articles to the editions before them, whose volumes their readers kept. An article they leave out was still in those volumes, so their bars say **Not in this supplement** or **Not in these new volumes** in place of the dash. The line above the bars counts the full editions and the supplements that have the article apart, so that the new volumes do not count as gaps.

## Each Edition's Card
Below the bars, a card for each edition names it and its years, then gives the longest article under the headword: its title, its characters, its characters per million of the edition, **Signed** with the contributor's initials or **Unsigned**, and its volume and page of the scan. **Open the Scan** opens that page of the scan in Pollis's own browser, beside the page, where the article can be read as printed. Any other articles under the same headword, such as a second person of the same surname, are listed under **Also under this headword**, with their characters. A full edition without an article says **No article**, a supplement or new volumes without it say so as the bars do.

## Reading the Text of an Article in Britannica Through the Editions
**Show the Text** on an edition's card opens the full text of its article below the card, a paragraph at a time, and **Hide the Text** closes it. The texts are those the Internet Archive read from the scans, with the running heads and page numbers left out, so they carry the misread letters of the scan; the plates, maps, figures and tables are not in them, and **Open the Scan** shows the pages as printed. The texts of all the articles come with PollisBritannica.jl as a download of their own, about 535 MB, which Pollis makes the first time a text is shown on the page: the card says **Downloading the texts of the Britannica** while it runs, a minute or more, and Julia keeps the texts on the computer afterwards.

## The PollisBritannica Database and Query in SQL
The page reads PollisBritannica, a built-in database that Pollis makes when the Julia REPL starts from the files of PollisBritannica.jl, and makes again when the package is updated. The page reads its tables `editions` and `articles`. In a duckdb> terminal they are `PollisBritannica.articles` and so on, and in Julia `britannica` once connected from the Databases view. The Next Steps group **Query in SQL** has four queries to start from: An Article Through the Editions, Longest Articles of an Edition, Articles That Grew or Shrank and Contributors of an Edition.
