# Overview

**Host a Game**, in Compose > Simulation Games, starts a simulation game with a class: the facilitator, usually the teacher, chooses a case, and the players join with its invitation, in teams that decide round by round. The case's model, written in Julia, plays each round on the facilitator's computer.

## What the Host a Game Page Does

The Host a Game page starts a game in three steps. **The Case** lists the cases of the installed toolboxes, each with its teams, its rounds and its briefing; **Choose a Case Folder...** adds a case of one's own, and **Get More Cases** opens the Toolboxes section of the Extensions view, where the toolboxes of cases are installed. **The Teams and the Rounds** chooses how the teams of the case are organised, when it can be organised in several ways, and how many rounds the game lasts, when the case can be played in different lengths. **Host** takes the facilitator's name, as the players will see it, and **Host the Game** starts the session: the chat opens beside the page, and the invitation is copied to the clipboard and shown in the step, with **Copy Invitation** to copy it again.

## How the Teams of a Case Are Organised

A case can organise its teams in more than one way, and the facilitator chooses one in The Teams and the Rounds. With **Board Members**, everyone in a team sets every decision of the team, and the last submission counts. With **Departments**, **Officers** or another structure of roles that a case defines, each player has a role, such as finance or marketing, and sets only the decisions of that role; the other decisions show "Set by" the role that sets them. Roles can have briefings of their own, which their players read beside the chat. A case whose teams differ, such as one buyer and several sellers, gives each kind of team its own briefing and decisions.

## Choosing the Number of Rounds of a Game

A case that names its round choices can be played in fewer rounds than it has, for a shorter class. The Teams and the Rounds lists the choices, the longest first, with the names of the rounds each one covers. A shorter game keeps the case's first rounds and the news that falls in them; the model is told the number of rounds chosen, so that it can end the game on time. A case without round choices always lasts all its rounds, and the step shows only how its teams are organised.

## What the Players Need to Join a Game

The players need Pollis and an Internet connection, but not Julia: the model runs only on the facilitator's computer. Each player chooses Compose > Simulation Games > Join a Game..., pastes the invitation and types a name. Until the game starts, the chat says that the player is waiting to be placed in a team. When the players have joined, the facilitator presses **Next Round** in the chat, which splits them into teams at random, within the case's bounds, and starts the game. The network must let sessions through, as for every Collaboration session: Compose > Collaboration > Test Connection... shows whether it does.

## Hosting a Game While in a Session

A copy of Pollis takes part in one session at a time. When it is already in a Collaboration session, a class or another game, the Host step says so, and **Host the Game** stays disabled until the session ends, with Compose > Collaboration > Leave Session. While the facilitator hosts a game, the Host step shows its invitation, and the page can be closed and opened again without ending the game: only leaving the session ends it.
