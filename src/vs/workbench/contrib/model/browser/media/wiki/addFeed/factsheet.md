# Add Feed — Factsheet

**Add Feed** follows a source as an RSS feed: a feed's address, a journal, a website, a YouTube channel, a PubMed or arXiv search, or the alerts a mailbox receives.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Follow a source that the catalogue of the RSS Feeds page does not have |
| **From an Address** | An RSS or Atom feed; a journal's page at Wiley, Taylor & Francis, SAGE, Elsevier, Cambridge Core or Oxford Academic; a website's page; a YouTube channel or @handle |
| **From a Search** | PubMed or arXiv, previewed before it is added |
| **From Email Alerts** | Google Scholar, JSTOR, HeinOnline, Nexis Uni, SSRN, Semantic Scholar, Web of Science, Scopus, Westlaw and Factiva |
| **Your Feeds** | The feeds added here, by topic, with Remove; Removed Feeds, with Restore |
| **Reading** | Every hour; the items are on the RSS Feeds page |
| **Menu** | Explore > RSS > Add Feed...; the plus of the RSS box on the welcome page |

## When to Use Add Feed
- Following the table of contents of a journal that the catalogue does not list.
- Receiving the new PubMed articles or arXiv papers that match a search.
- Reading Google Scholar or JSTOR alerts in Pollis rather than in an inbox.
- Following a YouTube channel of lectures or seminars.

## What Add Feed Does Not Do
The Add Feed page does not read the articles themselves: an item is a title, a source and a link, which opens in the internal browser. A search feed brings the results that are new since it was added, not an archive of past ones beyond the newest the source returns. A website with no feed, and a journal at a publisher not listed above, cannot be followed from its page; a feed's address found on the site can still be.
