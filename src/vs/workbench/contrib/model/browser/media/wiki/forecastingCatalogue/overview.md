# Manage Challenge Datasets: Overview

Manage Challenge Datasets lists the datasets on which models are scored and ranked, and the curated collections that gather them, in two tabs: **Datasets** and **Curated Collections**. Each dataset records what it is, the shape of its data, what its models predict, under which licence its data are given and how they reach the user. The page is in Compose > Prediction Challenges, beside Score Models, which runs a method of your own on the datasets made active here.

## Where Do the Datasets of Manage Challenge Datasets Come From?

The datasets come from their contributors, each a file of `[[datasets]]` tables. PollisCompetitions.jl contributes `metadata/datasets.toml`, the Dataset Catalog of the package, read from the package on this computer; when the package is not installed, the page says so above the table, and its datasets are listed once it is. An installed toolbox contributes the file it names, such as the datasets of its field. The datasets added with Add a Dataset come from the Pollis Workspace open in the window, each from its `dataset.toml`. Pollis keeps the schema of the catalogue, its fields and views, and checks every dataset against it. The column **Contributor**, in the views Access and Licence and Curation, names the contributor of each dataset. Two contributors cannot give the same id: the second dataset is left out, and the problems above the table say so.

## What Does the Tab Curated Collections Show?

A curated collection is a benchmark or an archive that holds datasets of the catalogue, or where they are listed, such as the Monash Time Series Forecasting Archive, GIFT-Eval and fev-bench for time series, TabArena, the AutoML Benchmark and TALENT for tabular data, or the Low-Dimensional Survival Benchmark for survival times. The tab lists each with its name, the year it was first published, a description, the number of datasets of the catalogue it holds and a link to it. **Expand** opens under a collection its datasets, each a link that shows it in the tab Datasets, and the papers that describe it. The column **Collections** of the tab Datasets names the collections that hold each dataset.

## How Do the Views of Manage Challenge Datasets Work?

**View** chooses which columns the table shows: Identity, Data, Access and Licence, or Curation. Every view keeps the Name of the dataset on the left, after Active and before Model Scoring and Saved, with its Topic as a tag under it. Switching the view changes the columns only: the filters and the order of the rows stay as they were.

## How Are the Datasets of Manage Challenge Datasets Filtered and Sorted?

**Filter by:**, beside View, chooses one controlled field: Topic, Data Structure, Geometry, Response Type or Delivery, or Saved. Under it, the values of that field show as boxes to tick, each with the number of datasets that have it, and None hides them. Ticking values of one field keeps the datasets that have any of them; values ticked in several fields must all be met, and Filter by shows how many values of each field are ticked. Clicking a value shown as a tag in a cell filters by that value too. Each filter in use shows as a chip, which a click removes, and **Clear Filters** removes them all. Clicking a column heading sorts the rows by that column, and clicking it again reverses the order; datasets without a value in that column come last either way.

## How Are Datasets Sent to Score Models?

The tab Datasets starts with the column **Active**. A tick makes any dataset active, as one you are working with, and the page keeps the active datasets for the next time it opens. **Score Challenge Models** opens Score Models, whose step Datasets lists the active datasets on which it can score models: the competitions of PollisCompetitions.jl that Score Models runs, and the datasets you add. Under the buttons, the page names the datasets listed there and those left out; the mouse over the tick of a dataset left out says so, and beside the buttons the page counts the active datasets and on how many of them models can be scored.

The column **Model Scoring** says whether Score Models can score models on a dataset: **Ready** when Pollis holds the values held out from its forecasters and computes its measures, **Not Yet** otherwise. A dataset sets how models are scored on it, which values the forecasters see, which are held out to score their forecasts, and by which measures, and that is what the mouse over **Ready** says: for a challenge of series, the last values of each series are held out, as many as the horizon of its frequency; for a tabular or survival dataset, the test rows of each of its published splits are held out, and the predictions of a model fitted on the other rows are scored by the measures of the dataset's benchmark, beside its published runs on the same splits; for a challenge decided in rounds, each round is decided from the prices up to its start.

## What Do the Details of a Dataset Show?

**Expand**, the button under the tick of a row, opens under it every field the dataset has, grouped as the views group them, and **Restore Size**, in its place, closes them. A click elsewhere in the row leaves it as it is, so that a value can be clicked to filter by it. **Source** opens the page the data come from in the browser of Pollis. **Papers** shows the full references of the works that describe the dataset, from PollisReferences, each with its DOI. A dataset of PollisCompetitions.jl whose data are on this computer has the button **Save to Workspace**, and a challenge that Score Models runs has **Open in Score Models**, which makes the dataset active and opens that page, where its first step lists it.

## How Are Datasets Saved in the Pollis Workspace?

**Save to Workspace**, in the details of a dataset, and **Save Active Datasets to Workspace**, beside Score Challenge Models, copy the data of the datasets into the Pollis Workspace, the folder of your own work, under `data/forecasting/`, one folder for each dataset, named by its id. The data are copied from where PollisCompetitions.jl keeps them on this computer: `data/builtin/<id>` of the package for a dataset built in, else `~/.pollis/competitions/<id>` for one downloaded, each with its `info.toml`. Beside the files, `saved.toml` records the size, time and SHA-256 of each file as it was saved, so that the page sees when the copy changes. Saving again a copy that changed asks first, and moves the old copy to the Trash. With no Pollis Workspace open, Save to Workspace offers to create one, in Documents by default, or to open one; Pollis opens it in the window, and the datasets are then saved again. Under the buttons, the page says which datasets it saved and which it left out, as their data are not on this computer. Browsing and scoring need no workspace.

## What Does the Column Saved Say?

The column **Saved** says where the data of each dataset are kept on this computer: **Built-in** when they come with PollisCompetitions.jl, in `data/builtin/<id>` of the package; **Downloaded** when they were downloaded into `~/.pollis/competitions/<id>`; **Workspace** when they are in `data/forecasting/<id>` of the Pollis Workspace, a dataset added with Add a Dataset or a copy saved there; and **No** when they are not on this computer, so that fetching them from their source, under Source in the details, needs an internet connection. A dataset kept in two places shows both, such as **Built-in, Workspace**, and **Workspace (Changed)** says that a file of the copy in the workspace was changed, added or removed since it was saved. The mouse over a place gives its folder, and, for a dataset whose data are on this computer but not in the workspace, says that Save to Workspace copies it there. The Pollis Workspace is the one open in the window, else the one Pollis remembers, the last one created or opened (the setting Pollis Workspace Location). Saved is also a field of Filter by. The column follows the folders as they change, without reopening the page. Beside the saved datasets, the page writes `forecastingCatalogue.toml`, the whole catalogue as it last read it, with the datasets of every contributor and the curated collections, so that Julia can read it with `TOML.parsefile`; the page rewrites it when the catalogue changes, and it is not edited by hand.

## How Is a Dataset of Your Own Added to Manage Challenge Datasets?

**Add a Dataset...**, beside Score Challenge Models, adds a dataset from a file of your own: a CSV, TSV or Parquet table with a row for each observation, one column naming its series, one giving its time and one its value. It opens a form whose **Choose File...** asks for the file; the form then shows the first rows of the file and asks for the three columns, the name and id of the dataset, its frequency, its seasonal period, its horizon, its topic and, if you wish, its licence. **Add to Workspace** checks the file and converts it into `data/forecasting/<id>/` of the Pollis Workspace, as `series.parquet` and `observations.parquet`, the files PollisCompetitions.jl keeps for a competition: the series are numbered in the order of their names, the values of each in the order of the time column, and the last horizon values of each series are its test values. Nothing is written when the file has a problem, such as a row with no number as its value, a time given twice in a series, or a series with no more values than the horizon; the form lists them. Gaps in time are not filled.

## What Is in the Folder of a Dataset of Your Own?

Beside `series.parquet` and `observations.parquet`, the folder of a dataset added with Add a Dataset holds `dataset.toml` and `saved.toml`. In `dataset.toml`, the table `[conversion]` says from which file and columns, with which frequency, seasonal period and horizon, the dataset was made, and into how many series; the table `[[datasets]]` is the dataset in the catalogue: a time series whose response is a number, with the number of its rows. Its fields may be edited by hand, as the schema of the catalogue allows, and references added as `[[datasets.references]]` tables, each with the `bibtex_key` of a work of PollisReferences. **Edit Facts**, in the details of the dataset, opens the form filled with its facts, and **Convert Again** converts the file again with them, keeping the fields and references added by hand; the file must still be where it was. The column Saved says Workspace (Changed) when the converted files change. The datasets added can be made active like the challenges, and **Open in Score Models**, in their details, opens that page with the dataset ticked; it scores them beside Naive2, by sMAPE, MASE and OWA.
