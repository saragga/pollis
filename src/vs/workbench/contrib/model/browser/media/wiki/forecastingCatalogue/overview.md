# Manage Competition Datasets: Overview

Manage Competition Datasets is a catalogue of forecasting datasets and competitions, and of the curated collections that gather them, in two tabs: **Datasets** and **Curated Collections**. Each entry records what it holds, how its forecasts are evaluated, where its data come from, what computer it needs and what it teaches. The page is in Compose > Forecasting Competitions, beside Score Forecasting Models, which runs a forecasting method of your own on the datasets made active here.

## What Kinds of Entry Does Manage Competition Datasets Hold?

The field Type of an entry is one of five kinds. A `competition` was held once, over a fixed period, with its forecasts scored on values the forecasters had not seen. A `live_competition` still runs, in rounds or continuously, such as a tournament or a competition that Kaggle keeps open for learning. A `dataset` is a collection of series published for research, with no competition of its own. A `benchmark_suite` gathers many datasets under one protocol for comparing methods, such as an archive of datasets or a benchmark of foundation models. A `forecast_archive` keeps the forecasts that teams or professional forecasters submitted over time, with the values that came true, such as a forecast hub of epidemics or a survey of professional forecasters. The tab Curated Collections holds the entries of type `benchmark_suite`, and the tab Datasets every other kind.

## How Do the Views of Manage Competition Datasets Work?

**View** chooses which columns the table shows: Identity and Provenance, Access and Licence, Data Structure, Task and Evaluation, Reference Results, Practical Footprint, Pedagogy or Curation, one view for each group of fields. Every view keeps the Name of the entry on the left, after Active and Workspace in the tab Datasets, with its Type and Domain as tags under it. Long text, such as Teaches or Reference, shows two lines in a cell and in full in the details of the entry. The Curation view adds the column **Missing Fields**, the number of fields of the schema an entry does not have yet, with their names when the mouse is over the number, and sorts the rows by it, most missing first. Switching the view changes the columns only: the filters and the order of the rows stay as they were.

## How Are the Entries of Manage Competition Datasets Filtered and Sorted?

**Filter by:**, beside View, chooses one controlled field: Type, Domain, Era, Frequency, Forecast Type, Package, Delivery, Status, Course Level or Size. Under it, the values of that field show as boxes to tick, each with the number of entries of the tab that have it, and None hides them. Ticking values of one field keeps the entries that have any of them; values ticked in several fields must all be met, and Filter by shows how many values of each field are ticked. Era is the period of the start year, before 2000, 2000 to 2014, 2015 to 2022 or 2023 on, and serves only to filter by. Clicking a value shown as a tag in a cell filters by that value too, for any field, such as a metric or a covariate. Each filter in use shows as a chip, which a click removes, and **Clear Filters** removes them all. Clicking a column heading sorts the rows by that column, and clicking it again reverses the order; entries without a value in that column come last either way.

## How Are Datasets Sent to Score Forecasting Models?

The tab Datasets starts with the column **Active**. A tick makes any dataset active, as one you are working with, and the page keeps the active datasets for the next time it opens. **Score Competition Models** opens Score Forecasting Models, whose step Datasets lists the active datasets on which it can score models, to score a forecasting method of your own on them: the competitions marked implemented in the package PollisCompetitions.jl, whose data it downloads, and the datasets you add. Under the buttons, the page names the datasets listed there and those left out; the mouse over the tick of a dataset left out says so, and beside the buttons the page counts the active datasets and on how many of them models can be scored.

The column **Model Scoring**, after Active in the tab Datasets, says whether Score Forecasting Models can score models on a dataset: **Ready** when Pollis holds the values held out from its forecasters and computes its measures, **Not Yet** otherwise. A dataset is not scored itself: it sets how models are scored on it, which values the forecasters see and which are held out to score their forecasts, and by which measures, and that is what the mouse over **Ready** says. For M4, the last values of each series are held out, as many as the horizon of its frequency, 6 for a yearly series and 48 for an hourly one, and the forecasts are scored on them by sMAPE, MASE and OWA; for M6, its 12 rounds are each decided from the prices up to their start. The view Task and Evaluation gives the split protocol of every dataset, its horizon and its metrics.

## What Do the Details of an Entry Show?

**Expand**, the button under the tick of a row, opens under it every field the entry has, grouped as the views group them, and **Restore Size**, in its place, closes them. A click elsewhere in the row leaves it as it is, so that a value can be clicked to filter by it. **Web Page** and **DOI** open in the browser of Pollis. **Papers** shows the full references of the works that describe the entry, from the References of the page, each with its DOI. **To Check** lists the facts of the entry still to be checked against their primary source, such as a licence, a number of series or a DOI. A competition that Pollis already downloads, marked implemented in the package PollisCompetitions.jl, has the buttons **Save to Workspace**, which saves its data in the Pollis Workspace, and **Open in Score Forecasting Models**, which makes the dataset active and opens that page, where its first step lists it. A curated collection lists the datasets of the catalogue that it holds, under **Datasets in This Collection**.

## How Are Datasets Saved in the Pollis Workspace?

**Save to Workspace**, in the details of a dataset, and **Save Active Datasets to Workspace**, beside Score Competition Models, copy the data of the datasets that Pollis downloads into the Pollis Workspace, the folder of your own work, under `data/forecasting/`, one folder for each dataset, named by its id. A dataset not on this computer yet is downloaded first in the Julia REPL, by PollisCompetitions.jl, and saved once the download ends. Beside the files, `saved.toml` records the size, time and SHA-256 of each file as it was saved, so that the page sees when the copy changes. Saving again a copy that changed asks first, and moves the old copy to the Trash. With no Pollis Workspace open, Save to Workspace offers to create one, in Documents by default, or to open one; Pollis opens it in the window, and the datasets are then saved again. Under the buttons, the page says which datasets it saved, which it is downloading first, and which it left out, as Pollis does not download them yet. Browsing and scoring need no workspace.

## What Does the Column Workspace Say?

The column **Workspace**, after Model Scoring in the tab Datasets, says where each dataset stands in the Pollis Workspace: **Not Saved**, **Downloading** while the Julia REPL downloads it to be saved, **Saved** when the copy is as it was saved, and **Saved and Changed** when a file of the copy was changed, added or removed since. A dataset that Pollis does not download is Not Saved, and the mouse over it says why it cannot be saved. The Pollis Workspace is the one open in the window, else the one Pollis remembers, the last one created or opened (the setting Pollis Workspace Location); with neither, the column says **No Workspace**, with **Create Workspace...** beside it. The column follows the folders as they change, without reopening the page. Beside the saved datasets, the page writes `forecastingCatalogue.toml`, the whole catalogue as it last read it, with the entries of every contributor, so that Julia can read it with `TOML.parsefile`; the page rewrites it when the catalogue changes, and it is not edited by hand.

## How Is a Dataset of Your Own Added to Manage Competition Datasets?

**Add a Dataset...**, beside Score Competition Models, adds a dataset from a file of your own: a CSV, TSV or Parquet table with a row for each observation, one column naming its series, one giving its time and one its value. It opens a form whose **Choose File...** asks for the file; the form then shows the first rows of the file and asks for the three columns, the name and id of the dataset, its frequency, its seasonal period, its horizon, its domain and, if you wish, its licence and notes. **Add to Workspace** checks the file and converts it into `data/forecasting/<id>/` of the Pollis Workspace, as `series.parquet` and `observations.parquet`, the files PollisCompetitions.jl keeps for a competition: the series are numbered in the order of their names, the values of each in the order of the time column, and the last horizon values of each series are its test values. Nothing is written when the file has a problem, such as a row with no number as its value, a time given twice in a series, or a series with no more values than the horizon; the form lists them. Gaps in time are not filled.

## What Is in the Folder of a Dataset of Your Own?

Beside `series.parquet` and `observations.parquet`, the folder of a dataset added with Add a Dataset holds `dataset.toml` and `saved.toml`. In `dataset.toml`, the table `[conversion]` says from which file and columns, with which seasonal period and horizon, the dataset was made, and the table `[[entry]]` is the dataset in the catalogue, with the number of series and their shortest, median and longest lengths. The fields of the entry may be edited by hand, as the schema of the catalogue allows. **Edit Facts**, in the details of the dataset, opens the form filled with its facts, and **Convert Again** converts the file again with them, keeping the fields added by hand; the file must still be where it was. The Workspace column says Saved and Changed when the converted files change. The datasets added have no Package. They can be made active like the competitions, and **Open in Score Forecasting Models**, in their details, opens that page with the dataset ticked; it scores them as M1 and M3 are, beside Naive2.

## Where Do the Entries of Manage Competition Datasets Come From?

Pollis keeps the schema of the catalogue, its fields and views, and checks every entry against it; the entries come from their contributors, each a file of entries. PollisCompetitions.jl contributes its file from the package on this computer, or, when Julia or the package is not there, from the copy that Pollis ships, so browsing needs no Julia and no download. An installed toolbox contributes the file it names, such as the datasets of its field. The datasets added with Add a Dataset come from the Pollis Workspace open in the window, each from its `dataset.toml`. The column **Source**, in the views Access and Licence and Curation, names the contributor of each entry. Two contributors cannot give the same id: the second entry is left out, and the problems above the table say so.

## How Is the Catalogue Related to the Packages That Download the Data?

An entry describes its data, but holds no addresses of data files and no checksums. Those are in the catalogue of the package that downloads the entry, PollisCompetitions.jl for competitions and archives of forecasts, or PollisDatasets.jl for datasets and benchmark suites, where each file is listed with its address and its SHA-256. The field Package Id of an entry names its id in that package's catalogue, so that two entries made from the same data, such as the Accuracy and Uncertainty competitions of M5, can share one download.
