# Media Coverage — News Sources

## Which News Sources Media Coverage Searches
The Sources of the Media Coverage page are ticked one by one, and the choice is kept for the next search.

| Source | What it lists | Key | Articles a search |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google News | Papers and sites the world over | None | At most 100 |
| Yahoo Finance | The latest headlines of a ticker | None | Its latest headlines |
| The New York Times | Its own articles | Free, your own | The newest 50 |
| The Guardian | Its own articles | Free, your own | The newest 1,000 |
| PR Newswire | Press releases, read while ticked | None | Those out since it was ticked |

## Google News in Media Coverage
Google News gathers articles from papers and sites the world over, among them outlets that have no search of their own, such as The Washington Post and The Times. Media Coverage searches it with the Words of the form over the period chosen, and shows its headlines and links only, for personal reading. Google News lists at most 100 articles a search, so a long or busy period has gaps: the Coverage chart then shows where the 100 articles listed fall, not every article written.
## Yahoo Finance in Media Coverage
The Yahoo Finance row of the Sources takes a ticker, such as AAPL, and lists the latest headlines Yahoo Finance links to that company. Its feed holds recent headlines only, so it adds little to a period long past. The same ticker gives the SEC filings that **SEC Filings of the Ticker** marks on the Coverage chart, which is why that mark needs a ticker in this row. A search may give a ticker without words, and then lists the headlines of Yahoo Finance and PR Newswire only, as the other sources are searched with words.

## The New York Times and The Guardian in Media Coverage
The New York Times and The Guardian are searched with a free key of your own, from their developer sites: **Get a Free Key** opens the page where it is requested, and **Set Key...** asks for it. The key is kept in the system's keychain, and an empty key forgets it. The New York Times lists 10 articles a request, at most 5 requests a minute, so Media Coverage asks for the newest 50. The Guardian lists 200 a request, and Media Coverage asks for the newest 1,000. Without a key, the line below the Search button names the source that was skipped.

## PR Newswire in Media Coverage
PR Newswire is a paid distribution service: companies and organisations pay it to put out their own press releases, unedited. Most of them are corporate, such as results, deals, dividends and products, but they cover every sector, from health to technology to entertainment. Its feeds list only its latest releases, so Pollis reads them every 10 minutes while PR Newswire is ticked, and a search finds only the releases out since it was ticked. A company's results often reach the newswires before its filing reaches EDGAR.
