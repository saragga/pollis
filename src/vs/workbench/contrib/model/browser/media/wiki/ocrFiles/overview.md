# OCR Files and Snippets — Overview

## What OCR Files and Snippets Does
**OCR Files and Snippets** reads scanned works, often old printed books in other languages, whose text was read from the scan by optical character recognition (OCR) and has misread words. It keeps a record of each work, shows each page of the scan beside its text with the words most likely misread highlighted, cuts passages out of the work and corrects them as snippets, translates a snippet with a local AI model you choose, and reads a snippet or its translation aloud. The page has six steps: Open a Work, Read, Choose Parts, Extract and Correct, Translate and Use It; Read Aloud is a card of Interpret in Next Steps. Until a work is open, each step shows its fields and buttons, and a click on one says what to do first. It opens from Explore > OCR Files and Snippets, below PDF Files, whose items each open the page at a step, and from the Command Palette.

## Opening a Work in OCR Files and Snippets
**Open a Work** takes an Internet Archive identifier or link, such as `brievenaanzijnbr03gogh` or `archive.org/details/brievenaanzijnbr03gogh`, a Project Gutenberg ebook number or link, or **Open a PDF...** for a scanned PDF on the computer. Pollis downloads the work's text and the place of each word on its pages, then writes the work's record with what the source gives: title, author, year, languages, a summary and the contents. Every field of the record can be edited under **The Work's Record** and saved with **Save the Record**. **Languages** matters most: the checks of the words use the dictionaries of the languages given, the language of most of the text first, as codes such as `fr` and `nl`. **Works** lists every work opened, with its title, author, year, languages and source; a click opens it.

## Fetching Pages When Shown or Downloading the Whole Scan
The images of a scan's pages at the Internet Archive are large: a volume of a few hundred pages takes a hundred megabytes or more. OCR Files and Snippets fetches the image of each page the first time it is shown, so a work can be read without downloading all of it. Under **The Scan**, Open a Work shows the number of pages, the estimated size of all their images and the free space on the disk, and **Download the Whole Scan** downloads every page not on the computer yet, to read the work offline; **Stop** stops the download, and the pages already downloaded stay. A PDF's pages are drawn from the PDF itself, by Poppler, when shown.

## Opening the Scan in Pollis's Browser
For a work from the Internet Archive, **Open the Scan**, under The Work's Record, opens the page being read as an image in Pollis's own browser, at the size it was scanned, in a tab titled with the work and the page. **Previous Page** and **Next Page** on the browser's bar go through the scan, and the page's number, typed in the field between them, goes to that page; the pages are numbered as in OCR Files and Snippets, from 1, and Next Page stops at the last. **Open in the Internet Archive** opens the same page in the archive's own reader. A work from Project Gutenberg has **Open the Source Online**, its page at Project Gutenberg.

## Reading a Work Page by Page
**Read** shows a page of the scan beside its text, line by line and paragraph by paragraph, with the contents of the work at the side. **Previous**, **Next** and the page number move through the scan; the printed number of the page, where the scan has one, is shown beside it. In the text, a word underlined with a wavy line is in no dictionary of the work's languages, and a word underlined with dots is in a dictionary but the OCR was unsure of it; the same words are outlined on the scan. A click on a word says why it is flagged and gives suggestions. **Accept as the Work's Word** adds a name or an old spelling to the work's own words, so it is not flagged again anywhere in the work.

## The Contents of a Work and Its Pages
The contents at the side of Read list the work's entries, each with its label and its printed pages, such as `1-5`, or `6-` for an entry that runs to the next one. A click on an entry's label opens its first page. **Starts Here** sets an entry to start at the page shown, **Remove** removes it, and **Add an Entry Here** adds one that starts at the page shown, with the label typed above it. Entries whose pages could not be read from the source say **pages not set**. Printed pages and scan pages differ, since a scan's first pages are covers and front matter: Pollis turns printed pages into scan pages by each page's printed number, or, for a scan without printed numbers, by the difference set once with **Printed number of this page**.

## Choosing Parts and Extracting a Snippet
**Choose Parts** takes one or more contents entries, ticked, or **Pages of the Scan**, such as `87-88, 92`, or both. **Extract** joins the text of those pages into one passage: the lines of a paragraph joined, a word broken at a line's end by a hyphen made whole, a paragraph that runs on to the next page joined to it, and the page numbers and running heads at the top and bottom of a page left out. The passage opens in **Extract and Correct**, with its label from the entries chosen, which can be changed before it is saved.

## Correcting a Snippet in Extract and Correct
**Extract and Correct** shows the passage's text, which can be edited, and below it **Words to Check**: each flagged word with its piece of the scan beside it, why it is flagged, its context and a correction to accept, the first suggestion filled in and the others as links. **Correct** puts the correction into the text, **Keep** leaves the word as read, and **Accept as the Work's Word** adds it to the work's own words. **Check Again** checks the text as it is now. **Find Other Copies** looks for other scans of the same edition at the Internet Archive, and **Compare** flags the words another copy reads otherwise. **Check with the Model** asks a local model which words do not fit their sentence, as suggestions only. **Save the Snippet** saves the passage as a snippet, with every changed word kept as read and as corrected.

## Translating a Snippet With a Local Model
**Translate** translates the snippet open into the language given under **Into**, as a code such as `en`, with the local model chosen under **Model**, run on the computer by llama-server without the internet. The snippet's paragraphs are grouped into units of a few hundred words, and each unit's translation is shown as it is written, beside its source. Each request carries the unit before with its translation, the unit itself, the paragraph after as context only, and the work's glossary. **Translate Again** translates one unit again with the whole snippet as context, for a passage that only later paragraphs explain. **Stop** stops the translation. The translation is saved with the snippet, labelled with its model and the date; a new translation into the same language by the same model replaces the one before.

## The Glossary of a Work
The **Glossary**, below Translate, holds the agreed translations of names and recurring terms of the work into the language chosen under Into: a **Term** as the work writes it and its **Translation**. **Add a Term** adds a row, **Remove** removes one, and **Save the Glossary** saves it in the work's record. Every translation of the work's snippets into that language is asked to use the glossary's translations, so a name or a term is translated the same way across snippets. A term can have translations into several languages; editing the glossary for one language leaves the others as they are. Explore > OCR Files and Snippets > Glossary opens the page at the glossary.

## Reading a Snippet Aloud
**Read Aloud**, a card of Interpret in Next Steps, reads the snippet open, or one of its translations, chosen with the buttons above, with a voice of the computer for the language of the text. **Voice** lists the computer's voices for that language; with none chosen, Pollis takes the first of them, and with none at all, the computer's default voice. **Pause**, **Resume** and **Stop** control the reading. The voices are those of the operating system, so which languages can be read and how well depends on the computer.

## Using Snippets: Copy, Cite and Send to a Notebook
**Use It** lists the snippets of every work, with their work, language and translations. **Copy** copies a snippet's text, and **Copy Citation** copies a citation of it: the author, title and year of its work, its printed pages, the work's reference key when the record has one, and the snippet's label. **Send to Notebook** opens a new notebook with the snippet as a Markdown cell, quoted under its citation, and a cell for each translation with its model and date. **Open** opens the snippet's TOML file, **Translate** opens the snippet at that step, and **Read Aloud** opens it in Read Aloud of Next Steps. **Add to Workspace** copies the work's record and snippets into the Pollis Workspace.

## Adding a Work to the Pollis Workspace
**Add to Workspace** copies the work's record and its snippets, small text files, into the `works` folder of the Pollis Workspace, one folder for each work, so that a project keeps its sources when it is copied or shared. The workspace's copy is the one OCR Files and Snippets reads and writes from then on. The scan's text, word positions and page images stay in `~/.pollis/works`, once on the computer. On another computer, the record names its source by a permalink: Pollis downloads an Internet Archive item or a Project Gutenberg ebook again from the same address, and finds a PDF by its fingerprint, at its path, in `~/.pollis/works`, or by asking for it.
