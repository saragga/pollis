\section{Ask Pollis in one table}

Ask Pollis answers questions about Pollis and about Julia: how to do something, where a menu item is, what a setting
does, what a panel computes, how a Julia function or construct works. It answers only from Pollis's own documents and
from the Julia Manual that comes with the Julia Pollis runs, and every answer names the passages it rests on, so that
you can open them and check.

\section{Asking}\label{sec:asking}

\subsection{The page}
\menupath{Help\gt Ask Pollis} opens a page with a box for the question and \textbf{Ask}. The answer is written as it
comes, in a few paragraphs, with a number in brackets after each statement: \textbf{[1]}, \textbf{[2]}. Under it,
\textbf{Sources} lists the passages those numbers stand for, each with its document, its page or its wiki and heading,
and \textbf{Open}, which opens the document at that page, the panel's wiki at that heading, or the Julia Manual at
that section, in the internal browser.

\subsection{Search In}
A question is searched where it belongs: one on Pollis in Pollis's documents, one on Julia in the Julia Manual, one
on both in both (\S\ref{sec:routing}). Under the answer, \emph{Searched: Pollis, Julia Manual.} says where.
\textbf{Search In}, below the box on the left, changes that for the next question: \textbf{Automatic}, the default;
\textbf{Pollis} or \textbf{Julia Manual} alone; or \textbf{All Sources}. It is shown only when the Julia Manual is
searched too (\S\ref{sec:settings}).

\subsection{Following up}
Ask another question below the answer, and it is read with the conversation so far: \emph{and on Windows?} after a
question on installing a coding agent asks about installing it on Windows. \textbf{New Chat}, beside the title, starts again with
nothing remembered. The conversation is kept only while the page is open.

\subsection{The model}
The model's menu, below the box on the right, chooses the model that writes the answer:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{On this computer}: the chat models installed on this computer, run by llama.cpp; the one chosen is kept
    in \file{pollis.ask.chatModel}, for the next time.
  \item \textbf{Cloud, with your own key}: Claude Haiku, Claude Sonnet, GPT-5.4 mini, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.8 Flash or Gemini 4 Argon,
    with your own Anthropic, OpenAI or Google key, kept in Pollis's secret storage. The key indicator beside the menu says whether a key is set.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Evaluate Answering Models...}, the last item of the menu, opens Evaluate Answering Models (\S\ref{sec:compare}).
The passages are always found on this computer, whichever model answers.

\begin{pbox}{What the questions are good for}
Ask Pollis knows what the documents say, and nothing else. It is good at \emph{how do I}, \emph{where is},
\emph{what does this setting do} and \emph{which panel computes}, and, from the Julia Manual, at \emph{what does
\texttt{@views} do}, \emph{how do I write an outer constructor} and \emph{what is the difference between
\texttt{map} and \texttt{broadcast}}; it does not run code, look at your files or know
what Pollis did on your computer. When the documents do not answer a question, it says so, and lists the passages that
came closest.
\end{pbox}

A wiki still \emph{Not written yet.} is left out: it would match questions about its panel with nothing to say. A
toolbox's passages come in when it is installed and go when it is removed.

\subsection{The Julia Manual}\label{sec:manual}
Every Julia comes with its documentation, as the HTML of docs.julialang.org, in \file{share/doc/julia/html/en} beside
the Julia itself, with the search index its search box uses, \file{search\_index.js}: each section of the Manual and
each docstring of Base and the standard library, with its page, its title, its text and its anchor in the page. Ask
Pollis reads that index, with the Julia that the REPL runs, so the Manual is always that of the Julia you use: a Julia
updated brings its own, with nothing to download. The developer documentation, on Julia's own internals, is left out,
as is the table of Unicode Input, the list of the \texttt{\textbackslash}-names of the Unicode characters, which has
nothing to say to a question.

The Julia Manual is a source of its own, beside Pollis's, and is searched too unless \file{pollis.ask.juliaManual} is
off (\S\ref{sec:settings}). A Julia installed without its documentation, as some Linux distributions do, gives no
Julia Manual, and Ask Pollis searches Pollis's documents alone.

\section{Cutting into passages}\label{sec:passages}

An answer can rest only on what reaches the model, and a whole document does not fit. So each document is cut into
\emph{passages}, small enough that a handful fit with the question, large enough that each says something whole.

\subsection{PDFs}
The text of each page is read with \file{pdftotext} (Poppler, through \file{Poppler\_jll}). A page's labels come from
the document's table of contents, which the compile keeps beside the PDF: the topic or section that is open on that
page. The page is then cut at its paragraphs into passages of up to about 250 words; a paragraph longer than that is
cut at a sentence. Each passage carries the last sentence of the one before it, so that a statement cut in two is
found whole in one of them. Page footers, the page numbers and the running titles are dropped.

\subsection{Wikis}
A wiki is Markdown: it is cut at its headings, and a section longer than about 250 words at its paragraphs, the same
way. A code block stays whole, and stays with the paragraph that introduces it. Tables are kept as rows of text, a
column's heading before each value, so that \emph{Default: 20} can be found under the right row.

\subsection{The Julia Manual}
Each entry of the search index, a section or a docstring, is cut at its paragraphs, the same way, and labelled with its
page and its title: \emph{Constructors \textgreater{} Outer Constructor Methods}. Short entries, such as the docstrings
of one line, are joined with those that follow them on the same page, so that a passage still says something whole.
Each passage keeps the anchor of its entry, \file{manual/constructors.html\#man-outer-constructor-methods}, where
\textbf{Open} shows it.

\subsection{How many}
The documents of Pollis make about a thousand passages: the written panel wikis most of them, the Guide about two
hundred. The figure grows with the wikis written and the toolboxes installed. The Julia Manual of Julia 1.13 makes
2,446: 899 of the Manual, 839 of Base, 703 of the standard library and 5 of its first page.

\section{The index}\label{sec:index}

\subsection{One DuckDB file}
The passages are kept in \file{~/.pollis/documents/PollisDocuments.duckdb}, on the DuckDB engine that comes with Pollis:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
documents(id, kind, title, source, menu, toolbox, version, collection)   -- one row per document read
passages(id, document, page, heading, text, anchor, embedding FLOAT[768])
collections(name, passages)   -- one row per source: Pollis, Julia Manual
built(name, value)          -- the embedding model, its prefixes, the Pollis version
\end{lstlisting}

\file{version} is the size and date of the file a document was read from, so that only the documents that changed are
read again (\S\ref{sec:fresh}); for the Julia Manual, the Julia version too. \file{collection} is the source a
document belongs to (\S\ref{sec:routing}), and \file{anchor} the place of a passage of the Julia Manual in its HTML
page.

\subsection{Words: the fts index}
DuckDB's full-text search extension, \emph{fts}, which comes with Pollis, indexes the heading and the text of each
passage:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
PRAGMA create_fts_index('passages', 'id', 'heading', 'text', stemmer = 'english', overwrite = 1);
\end{lstlisting}
The English stemmer cuts words to their stems, so that \emph{filters} finds \emph{filter} and \emph{routing} finds
\emph{route}. Common words (\emph{the}, \emph{of}) are not indexed. The index takes a second or two to build.

\subsection{Meaning: the embeddings}
An \emph{embedding model} turns a text into a list of numbers, a point in a space where texts that mean the same are
close together, whatever their words. Each passage is given its point, 768 numbers, in the column \file{embedding}.
With the default embedding model (\S\ref{sec:models}), that space is the same for more than a hundred languages: a
question in Portuguese lands near the English passage that answers it.

The model is told what each text is, as it was trained to be. A passage is given to it as
\texttt{\small title: <its labels> \textbar{} text: <the passage>}, a question as \texttt{\small task: question answering \textbar{} query: <the question>}.
The two prefixes are settings (\S\ref{sec:settings}): another embedding model wants its own.

\subsection{How big}
Two thousand passages of 768 numbers take 6 MB, and comparing a question with every one of them takes milliseconds.
No vector index is needed: DuckDB reads them all, every time, and so never misses one. A vector index pays only beyond
about a million passages (\S\ref{sec:open}).

\section{Finding the passages}\label{sec:finding}

\subsection{Two searches}
Ask Pollis uses hybrid search: BM25 keyword search and embedding-based semantic search, merged by Reciprocal
Rank Fusion into the top 8 passages, all on PollisDocuments.duckdb. Each question is searched twice:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{By words}: BM25, through the fts index, scores the passages that contain the question's words, the
    rarer a word in the documents, the more it counts. It is what finds an exact name:
    \file{pollis.agents.tierCells}, \emph{Snowball Search Map}, \emph{Install Skills}.
  \item \textbf{By meaning}: the question's embedding is compared with every passage's by cosine distance. It is what
    finds the passage that answers \emph{how do I make a cheap model do the small jobs?}, which has none of its words:
    it speaks of the Light tier of Model Tasks and Routing.
\end{itemize}
Each search keeps its best 20 passages.

\subsection{One list}
The two lists are merged by \emph{reciprocal rank fusion}: each passage scores $1/(60 + r)$ for its rank $r$ in each
list it is in, and the scores are added. A passage high in both comes first; a passage first in one list only still
does well. The ranks are used, not the scores, because a BM25 score and a cosine distance are not on the same scale. In
one query:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
WITH words AS (
    SELECT id, row_number() OVER (ORDER BY score DESC) AS r
    FROM (SELECT id, fts_main_passages.match_bm25(id, $question) AS score FROM passages)
    WHERE score IS NOT NULL ORDER BY r LIMIT 20),
  meaning AS (
    SELECT id, row_number() OVER (ORDER BY array_cosine_distance(embedding, $vector::FLOAT[768])) AS r
    FROM passages ORDER BY r LIMIT 20)
SELECT id, sum(1.0 / (60 + r)) AS score
FROM (SELECT * FROM words UNION ALL SELECT * FROM meaning)
GROUP BY id ORDER BY score DESC LIMIT 8;
\end{lstlisting}

The best eight passages go to the model, about three thousand words in all. In a conversation, the question is searched
together with the one before it, so that \emph{and on Windows?} still finds the passages on installing an agent.

\subsection{Pollis or Julia: routing}\label{sec:routing}
A question about Pollis would find passages of the Julia Manual too, and the other way round, since both speak of
packages, environments and the REPL. So each question is first routed: its embedding is compared with every passage's,
as in the search by meaning, and each source is as near as its nearest passage. The question is searched in the
nearest source, and in any other almost as near, within a cosine distance of 0.075: \emph{how do I install a coding
agent?} goes to Pollis, \emph{how do I write an outer constructor?} to the Julia Manual, \emph{how do I add a package
to my environment?} to both. When two are searched, the nearest gives five of the eight passages and the other three,
each source's passages found by words and by meaning as above.

\begin{center}\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.34\linewidth}LL@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{38 questions} & \textbf{Nearest by the nearest passage} & \textbf{Nearest by the average of the passages}\\
\midrule
15 on Pollis & Pollis for 15, by 0.045 to 0.246; 1 searched in both & Pollis for 15, by 0.037 to 0.117\\

The average of each source's passages, which would be quicker, was tried first, and set aside: Pollis's documents speak
of Julia so often that their average is nearer than the Julia Manual's to most questions on Julia. The margin of
0.075 lies between the gaps of the questions on one source: with 0.05, only 4 of the 8 on both are searched in both;
from 0.087, questions on Pollis begin to be. Before
the embeddings are ready, every source is searched by words. \textbf{Search In} (\S\ref{sec:asking}) sets the sources
by hand.

\section{Writing the answer}\label{sec:answer}

\subsection{What the model is given}
The model is given an instruction, the conversation so far, and the passages, each numbered and labelled:

\begin{lstlisting}
You answer questions about Pollis and Julia from the passages below, taken from their documentation.
Use only what the passages say. Name menus, buttons and settings exactly as the passages write them.
After each statement, cite the passages it rests on, as [1] or [1, 3].
If the passages do not answer the question, say so in one sentence, and name the passages that come closest.
Answer in the language of the question.

[1] The Pollis Guide, page 35, Orchestration > Model Tasks and Routing
Not every task needs the strongest model. Compose > Orchestration > Model Tasks and Routing has three tiers ...
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Why it can be trusted, and how far}
The answer can say only what the passages say, and each statement points to the passage it comes from: \textbf{Open}
shows it in its place. A small model can still join two passages wrongly, or miss the one that matters. When an answer
matters, open its sources. The documents themselves are checked against what Pollis does, release by release; an answer
is as current as the documents of the Pollis you run.

\subsection{Local or cloud}
On this computer, the model reads the passages and writes the answer without anything leaving it. A model with four
billion weights answers in a few seconds on Apple silicon or with a graphics chip, and in some tens of seconds on a
laptop without one, most of it spent reading the passages. A cloud model answers in a few seconds anywhere, and is
better at joining passages; it is sent the instruction, the conversation and the passages, which are Pollis's documents,
and your question.

Both are GGUF files from Hugging Face, downloaded into \file{~/.pollis/models} the first time they are needed and listed
in \menupath{Compose\gt Local Models\gt Models Installed}. They are the defaults, not requirements: the model picker

uses the same embedding model, so it is downloaded once for both.

Changing the embedding model computes every passage's embedding again, since the points of two models are not
comparable, and can change their length; but only once per model: the embeddings are kept by the model and the text,
in \file{~/.pollis/documents/embeddings.duckdb}, so a model used before, or evaluated in Evaluate Embedding Models
(\S\ref{sec:compareEmbeddings}), finds its embeddings there. Changing the chat model changes nothing in the index.

\section{Comparing the answering models}\label{sec:compare}

Which model should answer: a small one that is quick, a larger one that is slower, or a cloud model? \textbf{Compare
Models...}, on the Ask Pollis page, answers that with figures, for the models you choose: any chat models on this
computer, of any size, and any cloud models with a key, such as Qwen3.5 0.8B, 2B, 4B and 9B, Llama 3.2 1B and 3B and
Llama 3.1 8B against Claude Sonnet. The local models are those of \menupath{Compose\gt Local Models}, downloaded there.
How fast and how well each model does other work, statistics, Julia code, DuckDB SQL and summaries, is the benchmark
of \menupath{Compose\gt Local Models}, a page of its own.

Evaluate Answering Models is one page, opened from \menupath{Compose\gt Local Models\gt Evaluate Answering Models...}, beside the models
it compares, and from the last item of the model's menu on the Ask Pollis page. It goes in four steps, from the top
down: 1, the models to compare; 2, the questions, of the test set chosen, those ticked being the ones asked; 3, the
judge, a second judge if you want one, \textbf{Repeats} and \textbf{Run}; 4, the results of a run, the figures of
each model and then its answers. The page remembers what you chose for the next time.

\subsection{Fair by construction}
Every model is given the same questions, the same passages and the same instruction (\S\ref{sec:answer}), so what is
compared is the model alone, and its answers are those Ask Pollis would give with it. The passages are found once per
question, on this computer, whichever models are compared. A later run takes again the passages an earlier one found
for the same question, as long as the index, the embedding model and the settings of the search are unchanged: the
embedding model is then not loaded for it, and every such run is given the same passages, so that runs, too, can be
compared. The local models run one after the other, each alone in
memory, so that none is slowed by another; a judge on this computer is loaded only once they have all answered, and two
such judges one after the other too.

\subsection{A test set, scored}
The \textbf{Test Set} field chooses the test set. \textbf{Standard} is the one Pollis comes with (below), which a newer
Pollis may update and which cannot be changed. The others are yours: \textbf{Save As...} copies the test set shown
under a name you give, and \textbf{New Set...} starts an empty one, each a file of \file{~/.pollis/ask/sets}, which
a newer Pollis does not change; each change you make is saved as you make it, the ticks too. Its questions are listed in a table, with the topic of each (Pollis, Julia Language or Topic Panels), sorted by any column: tick the ones to ask, or none to
ask them all. In a test set of yours, \textbf{Change This Set}, below the table, puts them in three ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Made from the documents}: \textbf{Make Questions...} takes as many passages as \textbf{Questions to make} gives for each topic,
    \textbf{Pollis} (Pollis's own documents), \textbf{Julia Language} (the Julia Manual) and \textbf{Topic Panels} (the
    panel wikis and the documents of the toolboxes), 4, 2 and 4 at first, spread over the documents, and has the model of the \textbf{By} field beside it, the default model unless you choose another, write for each a
    question that only that passage answers, and the answer. This is RAGTools.jl's \file{build\_qa\_evals}. The
    questions are added to the test set, and kept.
  \item \textbf{Your own}: \textbf{Add} takes a question, its right answer and the passage that holds it, chosen among
    those found for the question. Under an answer of Ask Pollis, \textbf{Add to the Test Set...} opens Add with the
    question and the answer, for you to correct, and the answer's Sources to choose from.
  \item \textbf{Imported}: from a file of RAGTools.jl (below).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Delete Ticked} removes the questions ticked.
A test set is a file of RAGTools.jl: a JSON array of its \file{QAEvalItem}, each with four
fields, \file{source} (the document and the place in it), \file{context} (the passage itself), \file{question} and
\file{answer}, as \file{JSON3.write} saves them and \file{JSON3.read(file, Vector{QAEvalItem})} reads them back. So a
test set made elsewhere with RAGTools.jl can be run in Pollis, and one of Pollis read by anyone who uses RAGTools.jl:
\textbf{Import} and \textbf{Export} take and give such a file. Pollis comes with one, Standard, of 100 questions
written from the passages and reviewed question by question: 40 on Pollis's documents, 40 on the panel wikis and 20
on the Julia Manual. It is read from Pollis each time, never copied, so that every computer with the same Pollis has
the same questions and its figures can be set beside another's. Since each question keeps its passage, a question whose
passage is no longer in the documents is shown as such.

\textbf{Run} then asks the questions ticked, or all of them, of every model chosen, \textbf{Repeats} times, once by
default, and has each answer scored; its label says how many questions, with how many models. A run of 40 questions on
three local models and a cloud one takes some minutes with a graphics chip or Apple silicon, and up to an hour on a
laptop without one. It runs in the background, with its progress shown and how much of it is done, two charts below it of the memory free and of the tokens read and written until then, every two seconds, and \textbf{Stop} ends it with the
questions done kept. Above \textbf{Run}, a warning names the local models chosen, and the judges on this computer,
that need more memory than is free now, their file and about 1.5 GB more: such a model answers slowly and slows down
the whole computer, so close other apps first, or choose a smaller one.

Every run is kept, with its models, its judge and the Pollis it ran on, so that a run can be set beside an earlier one:
the same models after a new Pollis, or a new model against the old. The latest is shown; the \textbf{Run} field,
above the figures, shows an earlier one, and \textbf{Delete Run} removes it.

\subsection{Each answer, side by side}
Below the figures, \textbf{Answers} lists the questions of the run, with each model's rating and the seconds it took. A click on a question
shows its right answer and the passage it was made from, and below them each model's answer in a
column of its own, with its sources, the time to its first word, the seconds it took and each judge's rating, its
five parts and, on hover, the judge's reasons, or why a judge did not rate it; with \textbf{Repeats} above 1, each asking under the one before. It is
the close look: how the answers differ, on a question that matters to you.

\subsection{The judge}\label{sec:judge}
The judge is a model that reads the question, the passages and an answer, and rates the answer: how relevant,
complete, clear and helpful it is, and how consistent with the passages, the five parts of RAGTools.jl's
\file{run\_qa\_evals}. It rates against the passages, not against the answer written with the question: an answer that
says what the passages say, in other words, scores as well. The judge is the model of the \textbf{Judge} field, the default model
unless you choose another: any chat model, on this computer or in the cloud, Claude Opus among them; a small local
model judges poorly. A judge may favour models of its own family, Claude those of Claude, so a run can be scored by two
judges, the second chosen in \textbf{Second Judge}, say Claude Sonnet and GPT-5.5, each in a column of its own.

\subsection{The passages found, and the embedding models}
For a question made from a passage, the run also checks whether that passage was among the eight found, and at what
place. That depends on the search, not on the chat model, so it is shown once, above the table: \emph{the right
passage among the eight for 34 of the 40 questions, at place 1.8 on average}. It is the figure that compares embedding
models, in a page of its own (\S\ref{sec:compareEmbeddings}).

\subsection{What a comparison sends out}
With local models only, and a local judge, nothing. With a cloud model or judge, what each is sent when it answers or
judges: the instruction, the question and the passages, which are Pollis's documents; and, to make questions, the
passages they are made from.

\section{Comparing the embedding models}\label{sec:compareEmbeddings}

Which model should find the passages? \menupath{Compose\gt Local Models\gt Evaluate Embedding Models...}, below
Evaluate Answering Models, compares the embedding models on this computer on the same test sets. It goes in the same
four steps: 1, the models; 2, the questions, of the test set chosen, those ticked being the ones searched, all when
none is (the test sets are made and changed in Evaluate Answering Models, which \textbf{Change the Test Sets...}
opens); 3, \textbf{Run}, with the charts of the memory free and Model Servers; 4, the results.

\subsection{A run}
Each model chosen runs alone in memory, one after the other. It first embeds the passages of the index it has not
embedded yet, into \file{~/.pollis/documents/embeddings.duckdb}, where the index of Ask Pollis takes them from too:
each passage is embedded once by each model, ever, so the first run of a model takes some minutes and the next ones
seconds. Each passage and each question is given the model with the prefixes it was trained with, known by the model's
file (EmbeddingGemma, nomic-embed, Qwen3-Embedding, E5, BGE, mxbai-embed, Snowflake Arctic Embed), or those of the
settings for the model Ask Pollis uses. Then each question is searched as Ask Pollis would search it, by words and
meaning together, and among all the passages by meaning alone. No question or passage leaves the computer.

\subsection{The figures}
A column for each model, the best first:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Found} and \textbf{Place}: how often the passage the question was made from is among the eight that Ask
    Pollis gives, and its place among them, on average.
  \item \textbf{Found by Meaning} and \textbf{Place by Meaning}: the same by meaning alone, the place the median among
    all the passages; what the model itself does, without the words.
  \item \textbf{Question, ms} and \textbf{Search, ms}: the milliseconds to embed a question and to find its passages.
  \item \textbf{Passages a Second}: how fast it embedded the passages, when the run embedded any.
  \item \textbf{Memory, GB}, \textbf{Dimensions} and \textbf{Index, MB}: the memory its server took, the numbers per
    text, and the size of the embeddings of all the passages.
\end{itemize}
Below them, each question with the place of its passage for each model, among the eight and by meaning alone.
\textbf{Use for Ask Pollis}, under a model, makes it the embedding model of Ask Pollis, with its prefixes
(\file{pollis.ask.embeddingModel}, \file{pollis.ask.passagePrefix} and \file{pollis.ask.queryPrefix}), and makes the
index again, from the embeddings the run kept. The runs are kept in \file{~/.pollis/ask/Comparisons.duckdb}.

\section{Keeping the index up to date}\label{sec:fresh}

\subsection{When it is built}
The index is built by the hidden process of low priority that compiles the documents of Help\gt Documentation when the
Julia REPL starts, once they are compiled:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The passages and the fts index, from the documents that are new or changed since the last time, in a few
    seconds. This needs no model, and nothing is downloaded.
  \item The embeddings of the new passages, the first time Ask Pollis is opened and every time after. The first time,
    this downloads the embedding model, 0.31 GB, and computes every embedding, a few minutes on a laptop without a
    graphics chip; then only the new passages', in seconds.
\end{enumerate}
The status bar shows \textbf{Indexing the documentation for Ask Pollis}, with the passages done.

\subsection{Before the embeddings are ready}
A question asked while the embeddings are being computed is searched by its words only, and the answer says so above
it. Most questions about a named menu item are answered as well; a question in other words, or in another language,
less well.

\subsection{What changes it}
A new Pollis, a wiki written, a vignette compiled, a toolbox installed or removed, or another embedding model. For the
Julia Manual, a Julia REPL started with another Julia version, which reads that Julia's Manual, or
\file{pollis.ask.juliaManual} turned on or off. The embeddings are kept by their text, so after a Julia update only the
passages whose text changed are embedded again, in seconds. Nothing else: your questions are not kept in the index, nor anywhere after the page is closed.

\section{Next: Ask Pollis in every panel}\label{sec:panels}

A model in the cloud knows only the context it is given, and a long conversation with it is soon cut short. A panel
of Pollis already holds the context of its question: what it computes, its wikis, the packages that power it, the
methods it is about and the articles it cites. Asked in a panel, Ask Pollis brings all of it along by itself, without a
word written about it, and searches what is written for that panel's topic rather than everything.

\subsection{Four sources}
\begin{center}\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.2\linewidth}LL@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Source} & \textbf{What it holds} & \textbf{Of a panel}\\
\midrule
Pollis & the documents of \S\ref{sec:reads} & its wikis, and what the documents say of it\\
Packages & the manual of each Julia package, and the Julia Manual & the packages of its \textbf{Powered by}\\
Books & open books on Statistics, Machine Learning, AI, Simulation and Optimisation & what they say of its topic,
  found by its name and its Overview\\
Articles & the works the panels cite, from PollisReferences & the works it cites\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Each source is a collection of PollisDocuments, beside Pollis's own, and \menupath{Help\gt Ask Pollis} searches them
too, by routing or as \textbf{Search In} chooses. The instruction of \S\ref{sec:answer} then speaks of Pollis and the
methods of its panels, not of Pollis alone, and \textbf{Sources} names the source of each passage.

\subsection{The steps}
After the seven of Ask Pollis itself, four more, each usable when done:
\begin{enumerate}[start=8, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Packages}: the package vignettes, the same for Pollis's packages and yours, rather than the Markdown
    of their \file{docs/src}: a package is found by Ask Pollis by its vignette. They are those of
    \menupath{Help\gt Documentation\gt Package Vignettes}: the packages on this computer, in \file{~/.julia/packages}
    and \file{~/.julia/dev}, and the other packages in the Pollis registry, whose sources are fetched from GitHub; a
    package on this computer replaces the registry's. After each index, the vignettes not compiled yet are compiled in
    the background, one at a time, each version once, and the index is made again. A package you develop is compiled
    again when its \file{vignette} folder has changed. The page of each heading goes into a \file{.labels} beside the
    PDF, as with Pollis's documents, so a passage is labelled with its section. Without the Internet, the registry's
    list is the last one read.
  \item \textbf{Ask Pollis in a panel}: a box below \textbf{Key Points}, the same in every panel, from the scaffold of
    the panels. The question is asked with the panel's name, its menu and the first lines of its Overview, \emph{The
    question is asked in the panel \ldots}; it is searched in that panel's share of each source, with no routing, and
    the eight passages are shared among the sources, as the 5 and 3 of routing are. First with Pollis and Packages.
  \item \textbf{Books}: a catalogue of open books, each switched on by the user and then downloaded from its author's
    page on this computer, never shipped with Pollis unless its licence allows. A book's topic is found by search, not
    by a map of chapters to panels, which would not keep up with two hundred panels. A book of 600 pages is about a
    thousand passages, some 40 seconds of embeddings on an M4 with Metal, much longer on a computer without a graphics
    chip; so books are embedded in the background, one at a time. The candidates, each to be checked for its licence
    and its link, as a classic paper is: OpenIntro Statistics; An Introduction to Statistical Learning; The Elements
    of Statistical Learning; Dive into Deep Learning; Probabilistic Machine Learning; Convex Optimization; Algorithms
    for Optimization; Algorithms for Decision Making. Simulation is still without one.
  \item \textbf{Articles}: the works a panel cites, from PollisReferences: their abstracts at once, their full text
    where it is open, on arXiv or PubMed Central.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Still open}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What is chosen in the panel, its model, its data, its settings, could be sent with the question too.
  \item Unpaywall would find more open articles, but asks for an email address with each request: Pollis would send
    one only if you gave it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open points}\label{sec:open}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Your own library}: your articles and books, as PDFs, and the works of PollisReferences, are the next
    corpus. They would be a second index beside Pollis's, with a choice of what to ask.
  \item \textbf{Voice, images and video}: the default embedding model already places images, audio and video in the same space as
    text, so they can join the index without another model. Beyond about a million passages, comparing every one
    becomes slow, and a vector index pays: DuckDB's VSS extension, whose index on disk is still experimental, or the
    Lance format.
  \item \textbf{RAGTools.jl and PromptingTools.jl in Evaluate Answering Models only}: asking does not use them. DuckDB does the
    storing and both searches, and llama-server the models, so a framework would add little; the answer is asked for
    by Pollis itself, and the index needs only llama-server's embeddings, one request each. Evaluate Answering Models uses
    RAGTools.jl for its \emph{evaluation}, a set of test questions whose right passages and answers are known, to
    measure whether the right passages are found and the answers keep to them, and so to compare models and settings
    by their scores (\S\ref{sec:compare}); and PromptingTools.jl, which RAGTools.jl calls the models with. Both are
    Pollis's forks, which run on Julia 1.13, as in LLM Text Analysis. RAGTools.jl also has a \emph{reranker}, a second
    model that reads the question with each of the best twenty passages and chooses the eight again, better, at the
    cost of some seconds. RAGTools.jl could come into Ask Pollis later, for reranking.
  \item \textbf{Test sets in English}: the questions made from the documents are in English, as the documents are; a
    test set in another language would show how well each model answers in it.
  \item \textbf{The routing margin}: 0.075 (\S\ref{sec:routing}) was checked on 38 questions. A test set of Compare
    Models (\S\ref{sec:compare}), with the source of each question known, would check it on many more.
  \item \textbf{One stemmer}: the search by words stems English only; a question in another language is found by its
    meaning.
  \item \textbf{No reranker}: the eight passages are those of the merged ranks. A model that reads the question with
    each passage and scores it again would choose better, at the cost of a second model and some seconds.
  \item \textbf{Vignettes offline}: the vignettes of the registry are compiled from GitHub, so a computer without the
    Internet reads only those compiled before.
\end{itemize}
