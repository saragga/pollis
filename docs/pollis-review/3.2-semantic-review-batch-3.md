## Cross-cutting findings

- **Z1. Defaults that switch the feature off.** Several defaults make the model identical to a simpler one, so the panel shows nothing:
  - `dist` and `distSampling` truncated a normal at -Inf and Inf (fixed).
  - The extreme-value panels put the "tail" threshold at 0 (fixed).
  - The `dsge` RBC model had TFP persistence 0.2 a quarter (fixed).
  - `de` estimates the density of normal data, so every estimator agrees.
  - `ceval` and `cps` in batch 2 had the same pattern.
- **Z2. Word labels from `keys(lexicon(crps))`.** Dict order is not the column order of a text matrix. It was fixed in `dtm`, where `DocumentTermMatrix` carries `terms`. The same bug is in `com` (batch 2, not fixed there).
- **Z3. The optimisation panels are the strongest group.** `devol`, `dgo`, `ds`, `es` and `dp` build their problem in the code, compare methods at equal budgets over several seeds, and check the answer. Other panels could copy the pattern. The one hazard: the scaffold migration (commit `b997a6e1`) scrambled `ds`'s default code (fixed). A grep for the same corruption signature found no other panel affected.
- **Z4. Data-access panels (`ecb`, `edgar`).** They print raw values without a question. For an accounting school, `edgar` is where the period and comparative-figure conventions of XBRL filings must be right; three actions got them wrong (fixed).

## Panel reviews

### de: Density Estimation

**Sound: sound. Worth: adequate.** Correct bin rules (Sturges, Scott 3.49 sd n^(-1/3), Freedman-Diaconis 2 IQR n^(-1/3)), Silverman against LSCV bandwidths, a smoothed bootstrap, and AIC across families.

1. **Questionable. `de.toml`, all models.** `data = randn(100)`: the truth is normal, so the histogram, the kernel density and the normal fit all agree, and the AIC ranking picks Normal. The question the panel should answer, when a nonparametric estimate beats a parametric one, cannot arise. Proposed fix: a bimodal mixture, or a skewed sample (log-normal claims or incomes).
2. **Questionable. Action `fit-check` (model `parametric`).** A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against a distribution whose parameters were fitted to the same data gives p-values that are too large (the Lilliefors problem). Say so, or bootstrap the statistic.

### devol: Differential Evolution

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.** Rastrigin in 5D; jDE (Brest), SHADE (success history, Lehmer mean, archive, current-to-pbest) and epsilon-constrained DE are all correct. Comparisons with BlackBoxOptim DE and CMA-ES use the same number of evaluations and several seeds.

1. **Minor.** Bound handling by `clamp` pushes trial points onto the box faces; midpoint repair is the common alternative. State the choice.

### df: Distribution Factories

**Sound: sound. Worth: adequate.** The valuable lesson is there: families matched to the same mean and variance differ in their tails (Tail Weight).

1. **Questionable. `df.toml`, model `moments`.** It matches a Normal to a mean and variance, which are its own parameters, so there is nothing to solve. Proposed fix: default to Gamma or LogNormal (positive, skewed: claim sizes, durations), where the match is non-trivial and the tails differ from the normal's.
2. **Minor. Model `discover`.** With only `mean = 5`, just one-parameter families qualify. Add `var` so that two-parameter families can be compared.

### dgo: Deterministic Global Methods

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.** The six-hump camel function has two global minima (f = -1.0316 at +/-(0.0898, -0.7126)), and the Candidate Minima action finds the second one. There is a deterministic against stochastic comparison at the same budget. Box perturbation replaces restarts, because DIRECT ignores the start point. No findings of substance.

### diff: Differentiation

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.**
- AD against symbolic differentiation and finite differences, with the step-size error curve.
- Forward against reverse cost as n grows.
- Cobb-Douglas elasticities equal to the exponents, returns to scale and the MRS.
- The degenerate Hessian of a constant-returns Cobb-Douglas is reported correctly.

No findings of substance.

### dist: Distribution Functions

**Sound: questionable. Worth: thin.**

1. **Wrong, fixed (`a3e81fed`). `dist.toml`, model `truncated`.** The default bounds were -Inf and Inf, so the "truncated" normal was the normal. They are now [-1, 2].
2. **Wrong, fixed (`a3e81fed`). Action `moments`.** Distributions.jl's `kurtosis` is excess kurtosis (0 for a normal), and it was printed as "Kurtosis". It is now labelled.
3. **Questionable. All actions.** They assume a univariate distribution, so they fail or mislead for the multivariate, matrix-variate, product, mixture and convolution models:
   - `plot`, `quantile`, `cdf` and `qqplot` fail on the multivariate ones;
   - `fit_mle` cannot fit a `MixtureModel`;
   - the KL "Normal Approximation" compares a probability mass function with a density for discrete models.

   Use `{{?model=...}}` lines (batch 1, X5).
4. **Minor.** The Monte Carlo Check prints two means without the standard error `std(d) / sqrt(n)` that says whether their gap is noise.

**Proposal (thin).** The panel should answer: *how much does the shape of a loss distribution matter when mean and variance are the same?*

- **Default code.** Normal, Student-t(4) and LogNormal losses, all with mean 0 (the log-normal shifted) and standard deviation 1.
- **Actions.**
  - Visualise: the three densities, and their tails on a log scale.
  - Diagnose: skewness and excess kurtosis.
  - Predict: P(loss > 3), the 99% quantile, and the 99% expected shortfall.
  - Compare: a Monte Carlo check of each number with its standard error.
  - Interpret: "at 99% the t loss is x% larger than the normal one".

### distSampling: Distribution Sampling

**Sound: sound. Worth: adequate.** Every action puts a sample against the exact result:
- moments and quantiles;
- a KS test with known parameters, which is valid here;
- entropy against the mean of -log pdf;
- the right-tail ratio, 3.45 for the normal.

1. **Wrong, fixed (`98ffbf79`). `distSampling.toml`, model `truncated`.** Same default as `dist`: the bounds are now [-1, 2].
2. **Minor. Action `sample-size`.** It draws one sample per n, so the 1/sqrt(n) pattern is noisy. Average the error over 100 replications.

### dmap: Discrete Maps

**Sound: questionable. Worth: thin.**

1. **Wrong, fixed (`83da59c5`). `dmap.toml`, action `param-sweep`.** It printed the final x of each orbit "to map attractors"; for a chaotic orbit that value is arbitrary. It now counts the distinct values in the last 100 iterates: 1 for a fixed point, 2, 4 or 8 for a cycle, 100 with no period.
2. **Questionable. Actions `multi-ics` and `forward-iter`.** They print endpoints of chaotic orbits, which carry no information.
3. **Questionable. Action `sensitivity`.** The maximum divergence saturates at the size of the attractor. The slope of log distance against time estimates the Lyapunov exponent: about 0.42 for Hénon at a = 1.4, b = 0.3.
4. **Minor.** `u0`, `p` and `n` default to bare names. Hénon orbits escape to infinity for some (a, u0) in the bifurcation sweep.

**Proposal (thin).** The panel should answer: *how does a simple deterministic rule produce randomness, and where does order turn into chaos?*

- **Default code.** The logistic map x' = r x (1 - x), familiar from population and price-adjustment models, alongside Hénon.
- **Actions.**
  - Visualise: the cobweb diagram and time series at r = 2.8, 3.2, 3.5 and 3.9.
  - Diagnose: the Lyapunov exponent against r, with period doublings and chaos marked.
  - Predict: the forecast horizon at which two orbits started 1e-10 apart differ by 0.1.
  - Compare: the ratio of successive period-doubling intervals, tending to Feigenbaum's delta of 4.669.
  - Interpret: why chaotic systems are predictable in distribution but not in path.

### dp: Dynamic Programming

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.** A lost-sales inventory MDP with order-up-to structure. Value, policy and modified policy iteration agree, with the contraction bound shown. A Monte Carlo check of V*(0) comes with its standard error, and the panel covers horizon convergence and the cost of demand uncertainty.

1. **Questionable. `dp.toml`, action `grid-resolution`.** "Scenario resolution" varies N equally spaced demands on [0, 10]. That also changes the demand variance, from 25 at N = 2 to about 9 at N = 50, so the curve mixes resolution with a change in risk. Hold the variance fixed (for example, a discretised Poisson or normal), or call it "demand spread".

### ds: Direct Search Methods

**Sound: wrong. Worth: rich.**

1. **Wrong, fixed (`140a36af`). `ds.toml`, models `ps` and `gss`.** The default code was scrambled; neither model parsed:
   - the direction-set comments sat on the `if` line: `if the 2n compass directions +/- e_i`;
   - the acceptance tests had moved into the step update: `h = improved ? fy < fx : h / 2`.

   The code is restored: simple decrease with a kept step for pattern search, and sufficient decrease 1e-4 h^2 with a doubled step for GSS, both halving after a failed poll. The corruption came from the scaffold migration (commit `b997a6e1`); a grep for the same signature found no other panel affected.
2. **Questionable. Default objective.** The kinks of f lie along the coordinate axes (|x1 - 1|, |x2 + 0.5|), which is the easy case for compass search. A rotated kink, such as |x1 - x2|, is where coordinate directions stall and OrthoMADS's rich directions pay off. The panel should show that difference.

### dsge: Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium (DSGE)

**Sound: questionable. Worth: adequate.**

1. **Wrong, fixed (`cf0d9645`). `dsge.toml`, model `custom` (all three toggles).** The calibration was rho = 0.2, beta = 0.95, alpha = 0.5. For quarterly data:
   - TFP persistence of 0.2 makes shocks die out within a year, so there is no business cycle to see in the IRFs, moments or simulation;
   - beta = 0.95 a quarter is a 23% annual discount rate.

   The defaults are now the standard rho = 0.95, beta = 0.99 and alpha = 0.36.
2. **Wrong, fixed (`cf0d9645`). Action `cond_forecast`.** It was labelled Conditional Forecast, with a description of conditioning on a path, but it calls `get_forecast` unconditionally. It is now Model Forecast.
3. **Questionable. All models.** With a single shock, the variance decomposition is trivially 100% TFP. Estimation can use at most one observable (stochastic singularity), and `data` is undefined. `estimate(...)` is not MacroModelling API, which estimates through Turing with `get_loglikelihood` (task 3.4).

2. **Questionable. Action `compare-samplers`.** It compares raw ESS for 1,000 NUTS draws against 2,000 MH draws. Compare ESS per second or per gradient evaluation. Turing's `MH()` proposes from the prior (an independence sampler), which is why it mixes poorly; say so.

**Proposal (thin).** The panel should answer: *when does MCMC give a wrong answer, and how do the diagnostics show it?*

- **Default code.** The eight-schools hierarchical model in its centred form, with its known funnel.

  - Predict: the posterior predictive for a new school.
  - Compare: NUTS, HMC and MH by ESS per second.
  - Interpret: how the partial pooling shrinks each school towards the mean.

### embedding: Neighbour Embedding

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.** A correct trustworthiness formula (Venna and Kaski), perplexity and n_neighbors sweeps, seed stability, and a PCA baseline.

1. **Questionable. `embedding.toml`, action `feature-correlation`.** It correlates features with the embedding axes, but t-SNE and UMAP axes are arbitrary: any rotation or reflection is an equally good embedding. Colouring the embedding by a feature (Colour Scatter) is the right reading.
2. **Minor. Action `vs-pca`.** Residual variance over all pairwise distances rewards global distance preservation, which t-SNE does not aim for; say so.

### epiComp: Compartmental Models

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.**
- The formulas are right: R0, the herd immunity threshold, the final-size equation, the peak 1 - (1 + ln R0) / R0, and the SIS and SIRS endemic levels. The SIRS formula is exact, not an approximation.
- Vaccination above the threshold shows no outbreak.
- The intervention-timing action is a good question.

1. **Questionable. `epiComp.toml`, model `sirs`.** "Recurrent waves expected: R0 > 1": without seasonality, SIRS damps to an endemic equilibrium. The 160-day horizon is also shorter than the 180-day immunity, so no second wave appears. Run about 1,000 days and say "damped waves".
2. **Questionable. Action `beta-sweep`.** It stops at day 160, when near the threshold the epidemic is still running, so the final size is understated exactly where the threshold is.
3. **Minor.**
   - SEIRD's mu = 0.01 a day with gamma = 0.1 implies a fatality ratio of 9%; state it (COVID-19 was about 0.5 to 1%).
   - The actions always run SIR, whatever model is selected.
   - `endemic-interpretation` uses an undefined `gamma`.

### es: Evolution Strategies

**Sound: sound. Worth: rich.** Equal-budget comparisons over seeds: CMA-ES, xNES, dxNES, ISRES, ESCH and adaptive DE. There are a neighbourhood check and a dimension-scaling study.

1. **Questionable. `es.toml`, action `algorithm-info`.** It credits CMA-ES and xNES with rotation invariance, but Rastrigin is separable and axis-aligned, which favours coordinate-wise methods. A rotated Rastrigin would show why invariance matters.
2. **Minor.** CMA-ES with the default lambda = 8 usually stalls in a Rastrigin local minimum. Restarts with a growing population (IPOP) are the standard remedy; mention them next to Multi-Start.

### evtDiag: Extreme Value Diagnostics

**Sound: questionable. Worth: adequate.** The mean excess plot, Hill plot and threshold-stability scan are the right diagnostics.

1. **Wrong, fixed (`114b468f`). `evtDiag.toml`, model `qq`.**
   - The GP option's threshold defaulted to 0; it is now the 90th percentile of the losses.
   - The GEV option fits the whole loss series, but the GEV describes block maxima; the comment now says the data must be block maxima.
2. **Questionable.**
   - The Hill estimator needs positive losses (it takes logs of ratios) and estimates xi = 1 / alpha; say so.
   - In `threshold_stability`, sigma is not expected to be stable across thresholds (the modified scale sigma - xi u is).
   - The QQ plot's SSE has no scale or benchmark.
   - `losses` is undefined; state the sign convention (losses positive).

### evtRisk: Extreme Value Risk Measures

**Sound: wrong. Worth: adequate.** The McNeil-Frey peaks-over-threshold VaR and ES formulas are correct.

1. **Wrong, fixed (`a4955289`). `evtRisk.toml`, models `pot` and `tr`, action `risk_comparison`.** The threshold defaulted to 0, so the generalised Pareto was fitted to every positive loss, where its tail approximation does not hold. The default is now `quantile(losses, 0.9)`. That also makes the 95% VaR formula valid, since it needs VaR > u.
2. **Wrong, fixed (`a4955289`). Model `pot`.** The "99th percentile" is the 99% quantile of the excess over u among losses that exceed u, not of all losses; it is now labelled so.
3. **Wrong, fixed (`a4955289`). Model `rl`.** The GEV return levels need block maxima, and T is counted in blocks: with annual maxima, T = 100 is the 1-in-100-year loss. The comments now say so.
